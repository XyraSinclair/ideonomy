# practice-deep-ideonomy

Speculative fieldwork. Enter a subject as somewhere worth wandering: notice
an oddity, change the scale, follow a consequence. Let a discovery change what
you look for next. The pleasure is in distinctions that suddenly become visible.

Composes P1 enumerate, P5 dimensionalize, P7 typify, P10 map, P11 gap-find,
P15 vary, P22 refute, and P37 residue-seed. Preserve the target: **surprising,
usable distinctions in the requested register**, not merely a longer list or
a safer-sounding one. These are editorial checks, not empirical certification.

## A breath

1. **Find the live question.** Choose a tension with consequences: something
   people experience but cannot name, possibilities hidden by a familiar
   distinction, a transition whose intermediate forms are missing. State what
   an item *is* in one sentence. Declare the register: observed, analytical,
   speculative, invented-world, or a clearly separated mixture. Finish when
   you can name both a plausible member and a tempting nonmember.

2. **Make the first inhabitants.** Write a small, varied seed before imposing
   headings. Give each item a memorable handle and the distinction that earns
   it. Try an ordinary instance, an extreme, a reversal, and a case from a
   remote domain; follow whichever actually changes the field. Humor and
   beauty can carry exact ideas. Finish when neighboring items differ in what
   they let someone notice, imagine, or do—not only in wording.

3. **Let the list teach its own grammar.** Induce the dimensions from those
   inhabitants. Which distinction is doing unexpected work? What lies between
   two neighbors? What changes at another scale, in the opposite direction,
   under a missing precondition? Generate a second breath from those particular
   discoveries. Finish with a new member that the first framing obscured;
   record the actual change of question, not a claim of exhaustive coverage.

4. **Discover the form.** Keep an unordered list when order adds nothing.
   For a **seriation**, try different principles—dependence, reversibility,
   distance from experience, amount of transformation—then keep the one that
   makes adjacent contrasts teach something. Name its endpoints and explain
   one consequential adjacency. Reversal, branching, or a broken chain may be
   the discovery. An authored route is an interpretation, not a measured scale.
   For a **map**, state what each edge means and show a relation a sequence
   conceals: a fork, loop, dependency, incompatibility, or passage between
   levels. Use item identities, not incidental list positions, as endpoints.
   Finish when the chosen form supports an inference that the bare pile did not.

5. **Choose the next expedition.** Keep plausibility, importance, readiness,
   and generative promise separate. A doubtful possibility can deserve the
   next hour. Name a near-term lead and a longer-shot branch worth protecting,
   each with the question or small experiment that would move it forward.
   Prioritize the opening an idea creates, not its ease of enumeration.

## The sharpening check

Take the two closest neighbors and show a case that separates them. Try a
boundary case against the declared item type. Defend the ordering adjacency or
map edge claimed above. If the distinction collapses, merge or sharpen it;
if a speculation lacks evidence, keep its modality explicit rather than
quietly turning it into an established fact. If a criticism merely replaces
a strange mechanism with a respectable different one, preserve the original.

An independent reader strengthens this check; name whose judgment it was.
A self-read is a self-read. Record a meaningful rejection or revision and the
best unresolved seed. Do not manufacture a rejection to make the gate look busy.

## Pace and presentation

A **sketch** takes one breath and leaves its best opening visible. An
**exploration** takes the second breath above; use it by default for deep work.
**Cultivation** follows fruitful openings through further lists or maps,
returning to the parent field when a branch stops producing new structure.
Counts are a work bound, not a target to pad toward.

Lead with the artifact: a sharp title, its item type, and the list or map.
Place the interpretive axis, speculative status, and next openings beside it.
Keep the full admission history in the working ledger rather than attaching
an administrative essay to every item. The reader should encounter the
things themselves before the machinery used to judge them.
