THINGS ABOUT METACOMPUTERS ONE CANNOT BOUND AS YET OR AT ALL

NOTE: It is singularly difficult to give definite, or even reasonable,
bounds to the potential powers or possibilities of metacomputers. The
following list illustrates this. The obvious implication is that there

is something extraordinary--perhaps unique--about the promise of metacom-

puters.

1. Achievable intelligence.

2. Rate of evolution or self-evolution: either quantitative or qualitative.

3. Power to accelerate science and technology: e.g. achievable rate of new
inventions and discoveries.

4. Ability to add to human knowledge, or rate of accumulation of knowledge.

5. Accelerable speed or rate of being.

6. Growth of wisdom.

7. Potential goodness, importance, meaningfulness of existence, plenitude
of being, or omneity.

8. Potential evil and rate of growth of dangers that might be posed to man.

9. Potential artistic productivity and genius.

10. Attainable exaltation of beauty (i.e. of the level of beauty found in

the world).

- Complexity of psychology, personality, and behavior.

- Axiological complexity.

- Complexity and magnitude of thought and consciousness.

- Perceptual powers.

- Potential level of activity.

. Attainable physical powers and abilities, or completeness of control

over the universe as a whole.

. Potential prescience (knowledge of the future, or quantity, sharpness,

reliability, universality, temporal reach, etc of foreknowledge).

- Potential levels of motivation.

- Grandeur, intensity, and spectrum of emotions.

- Rates and scales of psychogenesis.

- Logical powers.

- Growth and levels of conscience and responsibility.

. Power to accelerate industrial and economic growth.

- Rate of advancement of civilization per se.

. Rate of transformation.

. Powers of imagination and simulation.

- Power to reconstruct the whole of history.

. Rate of growth and evolution of physical apparatus (ontorium).

- Tendency to transcend the world or reality as we know it.

. Attainable dimensions and powers of memory.

. Potential swiftness of perception, learning, thought, and reactions.
- Growth of the height and number of aspirations.

- Potential dimensions of sanity.

- Rate and measure of 'xenomorphosis'--or of departure from human, or any

recognizable, form and nature.

- Evolvability of the sense of beauty.

- Potential revolutionariness, apocalyptic aspects, or approach to deity.
- Attainable mathematical powers.

- Quantity of ways, and measure, in which one cannot bound them.
