ELEMENTS ANY SECTION SHOULD HAVE

NOTE: Each and every Section of More Human Than Man should ideally include
most or all of the following elements, designed to make the book something
of a best-seller. Examples of the elements can either be prepared in
advance of writing the Section--in the form of lists or notes--or should
be found present when the Section is afterwards checked.

Humor.

. Novelty, as perceived by readers.

Concrete illustrations of the abstract ideas.

Plausibility--something that makes the Section or its ideas plausible.

. Definitions of terms and concepts, wherever indicated.

Abstract ideas, complementing the concrete examples of things.

Continuities, things that run Sections into one another at their starts

and ends, especially via anticipations.

Dramatic matter, something that gives the Section high interest.

Progressions, things that noticeably serve to progress the book's theme

or thrust.

10. Reader instructions, matter that tells the reader things to do with his
imagination at any point or as he reads the Section. For example,
such instructions might begin ''But consider...for a moment,'' 'Who
would be willing to...?'' "What are the traits of...?'' ''Could one
peer into the future..."

ll. Memorable matter, something readers will have a tendency to recall

afterwards.
