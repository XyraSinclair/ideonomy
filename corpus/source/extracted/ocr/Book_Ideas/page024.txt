FALLACIES AND MISCONCEPTIONS RELATED TO A.1I.

. That creating an intelligent machine will perforce require understanding

and duplicating, or at least imitating, the human brain and/or mind.

. That there can be only one type, or at best a few--perhaps related--

types of minds or intelligent machines. (In reality there can be
xillions or infinities of different types, an infinite range of
types, an infinity of dimensions of ranges of types, an infinity
of higher categories of taxa of types, an infinity of different
physical bases, etc. Moreover, infinitely better minds and brains
than any specific mind or brain are possible.

- That in order to create an intelligent machine one must first--or ever--

understand it, either fully or partly.

. That to create intelligence in a machine one must program, or specify

exactly and completely, the form, content, or laws of the intelli-
gence.

That a lesser cannot give rise to, or cannot deliberately create, a
higher form or degree of intelligence or being.

- That GSdel's theorems forbid intelligent machines or the making of

such machines.

- That the human brain or an intelligent machine need necessarily be

very complex, difficult to understand, or paradoxical; that a more
intelligent machine or mind must be more complex, or different,
than one less intelligent--or that there need be any such
proportionality or positive correlation.

- That the human brain is the most complex entity known to man in the

universe.

- That the brain is necessarily more complex than anything man has

constructed to date.

That what is meant by "'A.I.'' is simply intelligence--rather than
embracing in addition the artificial creation of psychology,
behavior, being or life, etc.

That to create an intelligent machine one must first understand
the abstract nature of intelligence--or even what one means by
"intel ligence''--preciseTy.

That the mind, or human nature, is "infinite'' or transcendental.

That one can estimate the future rate of progress of the field of
A.1., or the distance of the achievement of true A.Il., simply
by linear extrapolation of past progress, or the present state,
of A.|l.

That any sharp dividing line must exist or is known to exist between
--or that any unidimensional gradient must exist or is known to
exist between and defining--the "mechanical" or "artificial and
the "organic" or "natural", the "animate'' and the "inanimate'', the
"intelligent" or "conscious" and the "automatic! or "insentient!',
the "human'' and 'inhuman'', or even the "spiritual! or ''mental!!
and the "'physical’'.

That we know--that anyone knows or has any real idea--what
"intelligence'' is and isn't, or what it is that we ourselves
have in mind when we use the word.

That a mechanical mind or being must, ipso facto, have some
mechanical or artificial aspect, defect, or limitation that
distinguishes it from ourselves.
