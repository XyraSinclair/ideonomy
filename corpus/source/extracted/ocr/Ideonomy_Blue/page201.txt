As for the possibility of a kidney disorder analogous to schizophrenia, Rewer

The most obvious is that the autonomous or heteronomous government of
a kidney might occasionally become pathologically split into the
equivalent of two or more disharmonious or even antagonistic or
diametrically opposed centers, processes, or forms of government.
A kidney must communicate with itself, and the lines of communication
that transmit queries, data, and orders may sometimes become so depressed,
locally or globally, that a loss of synchrony ensues. Perhaps there are
compensatory self-organizing processes, and in circumstances of a loss
of synchrony these can throw half of the kidney precisely out of phase
with the other half.
