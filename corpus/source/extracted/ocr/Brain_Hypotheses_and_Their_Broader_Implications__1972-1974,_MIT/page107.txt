If these are customary processes for the posterior or lower cortices (i.e.,
portions of the cerebral cortex), then higher cortices are able to modulate
(e.g., inhibit, sustain, intensify, pattern, move in time, complicate, direct,
compare with reality or themselves, delete, etc) these lower processes. At
the level of the lower association cortices it might suffice for the higher
cortices to hold ‘in the mind's eye’ the patterns in the lower cortices, so
that these are progressively compared with, elaborated in, and derived
from experience; indeed, this could gradually give the higher cortices an
imaginative realm and imaginative life based on what is learned from the
comparison of ‘held and seen’ experiences.

suppressing issue of the posterior cortices by arresting or prompting

transcendentally important.

The ability of the higher cortices to intervene in the constant--and
perhaps overly constant--processing of the lower could mean that a view of
the world and of oneself could be abstracted (or picked") as sufficient, and
that the brain could then concern itself with getting further oriented in,
and with making use of, this arrested picture. The effect of this might
perhaps be equivalent to the difference between a boxing match and a quiet
view of a lake, a flicker fusion versus a stopped disc, an external program
and an internal program, a rout and a society, a field of flora and a flower
in hand, a guess and a mathematical certainty, babble and language, a
dream or television show and reality, or the chaos and the order of the
world.

Evidently, if such a view of the world can be abstracted, then it can be
added to, and it can be packed in memory. A particular view can be placed
alongside another, and they can be compared in a leisurely but exact
fashion. Apparently the motor cortex would want to have such
abstractions, because it could ‘hold these in wait’ for the execution of
specific actions or reactions.

Serial views of the past would be alternatable, operable’, separated,
compared, etc only naturally, since certain wholes? would be appropriate
only at certain times. Furthermore, the motor cortex might not wish to
release these wholes ‘through entirely'--from the beginning, without
change, etc--and the neurons of the frontal cortex might therefore only
naturally become concerned with a realm of active thought, and with
exclusion, purpose, and planning.

If a perception could gain control over the active process of perception, it
could cause inhibition of anything analogous to this mnemic perception, so
that the seeming rapidity of perception would increase. Or holding a
percept in the mind might cause it to 'go round and round’, or to correlate
extensively with subsequent experience, so that description would intensify
by contrastive (i.e., determined by, and of, contrast) omission ad
infinitum, and by self-identification with all that is definite and clear in
that perception.
