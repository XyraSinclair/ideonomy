CAUSES AND EFFECTS OF ASYMMETRY IN THE BRAIN
Pat Gunkel

Since asymmetry is basicly interpreted in man as verbal v nonverbal, the first
question is what purpose asymmetry served in the lives of man's nonlinguistic
ancestors, justifying the eventual introduction of man's marked asymmetry? Of
course, it could be that asymmetry is qualitatively or quantitatively a product
of early man's beginning linguistic history, in other words, a product of the
use of language, or of some level of thought, conceivably derived from language,
profiting from accentuated asymmetry. A simultaneous question is some degree of
measure of asymmetry, absolutely and relatively, and its application to man's
animal ancestors, and the related but interesting matter of just how far back
and in what form, of sameness of side &, asymmetry goes in various animals: has
it a common value to, say, frog®s, with their asymmetric medial habenular nucleus?

In the matter of dominance and specialization of each side in its own direction,
it is worthwhile noting that dominance or specialization of just one side, say
based on left hemispheric extent of Turck's bundle, would lead to contrastive,
compensatory, and negative specialization of the opposite side, which we could
automatically call dominance of a kind, and, furthermore, any such asymmetry of
function, along with attendant specialization, would strongly encourage the nervous
system to restructure that side, within the limits of its abilities, both currents
of change leading to mutual adaptations and the refinement of asymmetry; however,
it may be that this secondary process was not very advanced,

Also bothersome is the putative absence of any intrinsic qualitative difference
in congenital or early unihemispherie or aconmissural men, insofar as one would
want to attribute any special signifigance to asymmetric structure and function;
however, one is inclined to be very careful in assuming that there are on hand
any cases at all in the literature where no interhemispheric interaction in early life
could have occurred and yet intellectual development is perfectly or variously
normal, Moreover, in this connexion, the question of commissural interaction
may be quite seperate from that of functional necessity of bihemispheric operation,
for the two hemispheres may achieve a kind of synergy merely from cancertion or
division of concerns at subcortic, eg diencephalic or stem levels, or in handling
the external enviroment; functional asymmetry is permissible even in these indirect
terms and there is no proof that this is not what the asymmetry is all about. Of
course, such cases might impugn ideas about the bases of specialization and synergy
resting imperturbably on direct nervous communication.

Related to all this is the puzzle why in a minority of human beings chirality
should be mirror-reversed and in the absence of accompanying functional deficit.

An essential doubt is whether we are even justified in presuming that
there is such a concept as “general dominance", As to the origin of any specializae
tion, did this come from extension or retraction on the dominant or nondominant side?
Inasmuch as there is any specialization, has there been analogous intralateral
specialization, perhaps as the very basis of the interlateral phenomena? If we do
succeed in establishing one, some, or major causes and effects of asymmetry, can
we be sure that there are not others, that the phenomenon is not general, that
throughout the brain or at least in some other structures there are increments of
asymmetry quantitatively or qualitatively hidden by the asymmetries that we have
identified, eg as dominant? Would the brain be as successful if both sides
developed the same dominant structures or does our success rest specificly on an
asymmetry and a superior~inferior relationship? How early in life does asymmetry
appear, and taking us from the ancestors presumed to be less asymmetric? Is
asymmetry, of anysort, but one of many incremental ingredients collectively essential
to man's intellectual superiority but individually inessential?
