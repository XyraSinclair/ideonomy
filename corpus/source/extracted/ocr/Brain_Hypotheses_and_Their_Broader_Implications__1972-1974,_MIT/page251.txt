CRITICISM OF NEURGCIENE
Pat Gunkel

The trouble with conditioning experiments, eg to see whether a certain animal
can generalize recognition of a triangle over rotation, is that natural conditioning
in real life is extraordinarily long, it takes the length of ontogeny in a sense,
and the given shapes are "euparallel" functions of each other so that it is the
average variety of natural experience, and also natural habit, that are critical.
These obstructive as ineliminable habits are extremely important. Furthermore,
the particular goal of a conditioning experiment may require, in the natural case,
a series of prior conditionings, so~called, and these conditionings occurring in a
natural wey (environment and procesg, for purposes consistent with the previously
evolved or innate structure of the animal's mind. Thus conditionings are not
carried out for the necessary number of trials, are not natural enough, and are
fatally presuppositional.
