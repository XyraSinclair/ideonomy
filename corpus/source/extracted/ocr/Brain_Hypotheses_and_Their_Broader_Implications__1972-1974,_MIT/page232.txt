POSSIBLE ROLES oF SOME CENTRIFUGAL AND INHIBITORY SYSTEMS
Pat Gunkel

What role have fibers from centers on peripheral afference and from the peri phery
on central efference? One is in bringing center and periphery into tonic coordination
with one another. Muscular afferents might serve the central registration of
externally originating forces, whether resistant or active; moreover, such afference
might serve guarantee, moderation, and univalency of central excitation, Sensory
efferents might serve the simplistic central control of the quantity and novelty of
efference, as in the equilibrial exclusion of afference upon the disequilibrial
*self-excitation' of association cortices in the process called thought, and, like
the above idea, the guarantee, moderation, and univalency of excitation. On the
other hand, it is conceivable these "reverse" axons could serve the construction
of preferred single normal or absolute So-called ‘'filters'--such might be the role
of the cerebellum, in fact. It may be imagined that two states are of value eg in
setting of afferent stages: resistance to change to serve the cumulation or
interaction of afferent pulses across time, and maximization of change (as by
inhibitory or excitory freeing of such cumilative interactive patterns; or the
institution of some patterns could serve the exclusion of analogous patterns,
and conversely, the exclusion of inanalogous patterns by *self-selection'); it is
theoretically possible that changes in function at these points could have served
phylogenetic revolution, eg by maximal habituation or, conversely, minimal peripheral
habituation in order to effect maximal central differentiation and discrimination.
