Types of order. The amount of order in the world is so overwhelming,
interpenetrating, and interrelated that it simplifies and may go a long
way toward explaining what nervous systems do. I suggested at least

71 types of primary order, applicable to any sense, any nervous activity,
and any pert of the world.

Higher order order. I found that the types of order that I had suggested
readily and totally combine with each other and describe thereby even more
satisfactorily the form or, to use Fritz Zwicky's term, morphology of the
world. The argument for the simplification of nervous activity holds

a fortiori. The binaries I calied "second order order™ (not secondary
order, as that would be too pretentious) and I discovered that these were
extraordinarily useful in illuminating the pattern of the world about me
when applied to scenery, to sounds, to feelings, and to actions. Even
the “third order orders" seemed simply understandable and individually
valuable. Beyond, “n-order orders" frightened the imagination. I
hypothesized that, in an extraordinarily complex way, the types of order
and the world itself would begin to collapse at higher order orders into
things that I described topologically as "rings"™ or "n-order rings".

Reticulum(a). A or part of a so»called reticular formation, ie a tissue
characterized as its dominant feature by an extreme variety of apparently
random connexions with itself and perhaps with other tissues and otherwise
absence of any apparent types and regularities of structure, at least,
speaking relatively. The advantage of the term is simply that it is less
cumbersome and is more direct. There are others who use the substitution.

Stoichiogeny. The genesis of informational patterns in a tissue reflecting
the pattern of that tissue, another tissue, or, particularly, of the course
of sensation or structure of the external world, Since the deposited
elements could be of many kinds, the term does not prejudice the discussion;
eg the deposits could be abstract, concrete, or distributed.

Geocamptic stoichiogeny. What elements arise in a tissue may be assumed to
largely or wholly reflect the structure of the external world or of the

types of order proper to the functional tissue as a segment of the world,

I use the concept to suggest the passivity, triviality, and inevitability

of the process and the unwarrantedness of elaborate suppositions about genetic
inheritance.

Autocorrelation. Within a tissue, information may be correlated with itself,
eg for the progressive derivation of generalities. This applies, as well,
to the tendency of tissue functions, having underlying regularity, to become
integrated in the tissue, which may be advantageous for the extraction
subsequently of the order in experience. An initial wavetrain may be
correlated with itself and the subsequent pattern of correlations used as a
standard for "transcorrelations",

Integrocorrelation. Those two processes of autocorrelation may be taken to
extremes for the generalization of any information in the reception, in the
tissue, and between the two. This generalized pattern may be distributed
fully over the extent, spatial detail, and functional complexity of the tissue,
and in this process the tissue may be made peculiarly responsive.
