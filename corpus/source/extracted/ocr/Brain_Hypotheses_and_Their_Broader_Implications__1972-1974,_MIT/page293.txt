THE DESCRIPTION OF THE NEURON AND THE BIDJEPRINT FOR ITS SYNTHESIS AND PRODUCTI ON
Pat Gunkel

The subject requires a considerable presentation for the sake of details,
ranges, arbitrary sophistications, variants, criteria, tests, all possible
types, and so forth. Nevertheless, man can probably now build neurons and
assemble them in very impressive nervous systems. Should that not be so, man
is hours from that day.

Realistically, there is no need to imagine or build neurons with branches
and so forth. Instead needs may be stated and met by forthcoming engineering.
So eg groups of cells may be stated to be in relation with one another in
certain explicit densities, the relations may be arranged to variously mimic
what we associate with neurons, so we may imagine some definably loose order
of circular and open paths between abstract functions, orders based (and
together ordered) in terms of priority, relative importance, positive and/or
negative step functions, additions, mltiplications, and the like. Operations
may be stated for regular and causal transpositions of relations. Schedules and
hierarchies in time of relations, such as slow operations and arbitrarily great
transformational *maps', may be stated and easily functionalized. In accord
with the nondigital bases of nervous systems, analogue ranges may be inserted
on the "mapped surfaces” pertaining to explicit operations. If it is imagined
that individual neurons are capable of storing different codings like in a big
warehouse, the nature of output and input mst be specified, and a suitable
memory may be stated; I am skeptical of any such need, believing the demonstrated
complexity and variety of codes associable with single neurons to be distributions
between a@ifferent neurons with som correspondent coding lying in cell architecture
and physiology, but not being problematic; however, it places no great strain on
the imagination to visualize molecular mechanisms embedded in the unit membrane
serving massive storage of fasteretrievable memories, and their requirement
could put quite a multiplier on the economics of an artificial brain,approaching
man. Certain simple patterns, eg a universal sum, could be stated as the criterion
for a defined interrelation of this unit with other cells, and operations (but
not mechanisms) of distribution could be stated, eg under various equilibrial
criteria for an abstract tissue. An ontogeny and praxis could be stated as
operations involving ordinals, cardinals, transpositions, expansions of mapped
surfaces, or anything else particularly necessary or interesting. This completes
the first depiction of the makeup of an artificial and formal neuron as competent
as man's.
