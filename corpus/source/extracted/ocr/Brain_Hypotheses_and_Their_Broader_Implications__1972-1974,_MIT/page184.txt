THE BIRTH OF GNIGLOGY
Pat Gunkel

A major purpose in writing this book was to found a science of ontology,
a word derived from the Greek root ont- meaning existence, being, life, or
organism, as I understand. J borrowed the philosophic term because
radically the word is so explicit, corresponds so well with what is actually
happening in science and the world, and is vastly superior to narrower
definitions of a science, eg “artificial intelligence” or *artificial mind”,
whose use has been and would be unfortunate in leading to misunderstanding
and deformation of scientific work and thought, eg through erroneous
constriction and misemphasization. Moreover, the history of the race, the
form of the modern world, and the shadows of things to come strongly limn
the critical need for such an explicit and self-serious science. The science
will be both pure and applied, and, in fact, the very nature of the word
ontology bridges the two, eg transtemporally. In the external world, it is
apparent that science, thought, play, reason, emotion, idea, value, immediacy,
eternity“ possibility, probability, necessity, will, fate, individual and
collective, pure and applied, technical and arbitrary or casual, whole and
part, one thing and another, biology and psychology, psychology and philosophy,
opposites in general, cause and effect, the whole of tims, the whole of
everything, é are coming together as the major theme of the age and of human
history or intelligence, It is apparent that the nature and safety of the
world are confused and that the explicit need for description, explanation,
solution, and address of the world’s (often derived) problems with varying
urgency call for the answers of this proposed science. Accordingly and also,
it is evident that the science is not only timely but also imaginable, feasible,
formiable, incipient, absent, catalytic and perfect, applicable, gradable,
and emergent in my and others’ work, as well as perfectible and proportionate.
So the term paradoxically fuses all these things, eg possible and actual being,
subjective and objective being, theoretical and experimental science, science
for its own and for man's sake, individual and universal purpose, and the
division of human interest; a particularly interesting example is the fusion
or confusion of imagination and experience, ideal and real, abstract and concrete,
passive and active, é&c. The motivation for the science is in the conviction of
the superiority of a tertiary position to warring dichotomies in the patterned
behavior of the human race, or many tertiary positions to many false or misguided
dichotomies; the irony of the science lies in its fascination with the dichotomies.
It can be argued that the world is unnaturally complex and the mind of humanity
too analytic nowadays, and that convergence, synthesis, analyticosynthesis, or
universality is due, contingent, or inevitable. Another reason I had for
proclaiming an ontology is to revolutionize, define, correct, and protect the
motives for neuroscience et al in the future, eg liberating neuroscience fran
narrow pathic, pure or applied, institutional, natural, economic, mundane,
traditional, anthropocentric, experimental, & orientations present in the past,
dominant in the present, and possible in the future. It is, furthermore, my
conviction that we now have the ability to define, precise, and quantitate the
nature, goals, prospects, and good of the class of living beings including
ourselves and our inseperable progeny
