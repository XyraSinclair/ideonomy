APPARENT FUNCTIONS OF SUCCESSIONS OF LAYERS AND CENTERS IN NERVOUS SYSTEMS
Pat Gunkel

The view which I have, that the inherited complexity and the functional
complexity of nervous systems is not but simple, enables a priori discussion of
likely functions homogeneous and heterogeneous with successions of layers and
centers. It is an open question what any such discussion will neglect and the
relative importance of the neglected. But I have thought that the mind giving
itself energetically can race ahead of evolutionary differentiations that exist
in fact but are yet to be uncovered. To digress for a moment, it might be said
that the eventual comprehension of the nervous system will not be due to a simple
and ambiguous specification of the unique and profound case from accumulated fact
but will owe itself in part or whole to the flight of the imagination and the
brilliance of the human mind; the point is illustrated by the lines of futility
reached where sheer methods and just computers have been used to accelerate and
advance various problems in science but have always arrived at things the nature
of which is invisible to methods and computers and only intuitively knowable, s0
that the goal of artificial intelligence $s to raise the level of intelligence
toward man that the mere body of facts, however enormous and wide, is reducible,
extrapolable, and circumventable and the true shape of the situation can emerge.
This is the argument for theory, over and beyond superfluous fact, so that we
look now or in the future for theory free, equal, or superior to make sense of
nervous systems.

When we look to laminate structures we see that typically the explanation
for lamination lies in an alternation of cell bodies and cell fibers or ina
division of types of cells. One reason for this could well be the intensive one
that in this way extraneous influences and interferences can be eliminated in
favor of concentrated functions that may gain transcendentally by enabling
electrotonie episynaptic interactions, an exclusive chemical milieu, certain

synaptic regimes, certain electrical signs, a brevity of intercellular flow, a
bypassing of cell bodies in repeated interactions, a placing of synapses upon
synapses, a regularity of function, &, or the reverse (since these remarks are
mainly directed to neuropile) since attention may be given seperately to the
dense perikarya. Then too, the lamination may relate considerations nonneuronal
and yet analogous to the foregoing. Furthermore, within the lamination adjacent
layers may both be neuronal and perikaryal or neuropilar and yet there be a
subdifferentiation based on specialization of cell types (size, shape, chemistry,
sign, connexions within and without the matter) or of parts thereof (fibers,
synapses, axons vs dendrites, parts of the latter, state of myelination, &&)
But these things for what purpose?

Inversion or complication of sign, staging of flow, complication of processing,

(eg different cell properties"), opportunity for larger operations (eg amplifica-
tion of frequency, effect amplitude, latency, spread, analysis, interconnectional

tion), &. Successive or at least seperate layers or centers might be concerned

specific organization of behavior to be, however difficultly, found in the overt
and covert intricacy of the brain, with or without a bearing on these abstract ideas
in cases and in general.
