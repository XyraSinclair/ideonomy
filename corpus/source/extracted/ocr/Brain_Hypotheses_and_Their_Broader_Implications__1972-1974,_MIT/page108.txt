INote the idea of a hierarchy of operations, being able to control one's controls, instead
of being controlled by them, etc. - What is more characteristic of Man than his
“externalism"? Also note the difference between simply experiencing an operation--that
it occurs, and seeing the operation--how it occurs.

2What is fascinating here is the ability to abstract--and hence to catalog, array, and
use--'wholes’. Again, the animal merely undergoes experience; Man is surprised by,
criticizes, and uses--even husbands--experience.

This freeness respecting experience means that Man extricates himself from
systems of illusion and from a false and total cohesion of experience. Possibly the animal
is confined to the analysis of immediate and/or past things, whereas Man is able to do one
and/or the other and superlatively. For example, Man may be able to analyze things at the
very moment they are occurring; and such a special ability may be a catalyst for real

conceptual development and cognitive purpose.
Such an abstraction of wholes would lead to a very different mental topology.

The foregoing is a random chapter from Patrick Michael Gunkel's
book Brain Hypoth Their Broader Implications, which was
