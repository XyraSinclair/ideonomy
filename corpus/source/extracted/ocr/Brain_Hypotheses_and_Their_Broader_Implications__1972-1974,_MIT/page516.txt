Many of these items are interrelated. Some items are phenomenal compared to
others more essential. The twenty left terms might apply more generally to
brains. Seperately and together, the terms imply important qualitative and
great quantitative differences between men and machines; clearly the difference
is radical and this may account for the impotence of current computers.
Different animals will compare differently and unequally on the different items,
skewed away from man and computer. The items raise the many problems of how to
mechanize the many aspects of biological intelligence, and of at what thresholds
of covariant cross-over intellectual functions similar to ours may be found.
It is, however, comforting to have the set of considerations baldly presented.
Possibly we shall never know the cross-overs on a purely theoreticalujasis and
shall have to find, define, and implement them (even painfully) empirically; but,
at once, we may succeed with some cross-overs purely theoretically, or with some
values on these cross-overs. It is interesting to what extent the current type
of computer could be extended to more nearly match brains on the items.
