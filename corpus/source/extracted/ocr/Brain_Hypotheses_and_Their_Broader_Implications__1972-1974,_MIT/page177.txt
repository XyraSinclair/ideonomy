Turning to problems on the microscopic scale, it is clear that the engineering
of suitable components may be very difficult in the end and in the beginning.
In addition to the needs for pohogrernig spontaneous, and analogue components
(or points at which convergent, pluralistic, and unending processing will occur),
there may have to be properties of eventual partial or complete erasure (eg so
that the pattern of the whole tissue can transform and thereby go in arbitrary
and progressive directions), or of graded~'palimpsestic' erasure. Indeed, when
all the differences between computers and brains are considered one is inclined
to wonder whether the first and intermediate “artificial minds” may
not in fact, perforee, employ biological cells, modified biological cells, or
new biological cells (an idea I had viewed with contempt when I entered the field).
But the situation may be the familiar one wherein there is a naive pessimism
about possibilities prior to their exploration and a successful issue.
