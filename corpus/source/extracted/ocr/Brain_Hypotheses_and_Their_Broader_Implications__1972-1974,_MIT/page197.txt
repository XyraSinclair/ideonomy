The diadichotomization and diadifferentiation methods would both seem to
afford excellent means for recognizing sonie form, per se, or independent
of frequency shifts of the whole sound when the internal frequency relations
are preserved, and it is clear that a method of this kind is needed in, say,
Speech research. Applied to an audiospectrogram not or only uniformly
extended in time, the morphologic relationships ought be quickly extracted,
both intrinsiely, and extrinsicly compared with a set of unlike sounds, and
the morphic invariance is easily applicable to sounds which are in fact
complexly extended in time. Moreover, the method, and its probabilistic and
analytic caleuli, are such that its sheer concern with morphology and the
incidence of variations therein, that intersecting contima ought perhaps
allow the discrimination of those morphologic complexities, accidents,
variants, and subtleties that are our natural experience and defined
capacities the methods, to return to the plural, are methods for quantifying
relative and internal form par excellence. My point about the applicability
of these methods to making a machine dance any dance--essentially perform any
movement~-is relevant here in the world of sound: it is evident that the
Supreme uniquity of the method lies in its cambination of randan filtering
with a method of successive and intermediate contrast, which (with certain
further caleuli producing a spectral hierarchy) allows and convergently causes
the cosurfacing of all transformational singularities and simplicities,
regardless of their "intellectual brilliance", ie the method is universal
for intelligence (or at least it appears that this may well be the case). I
know of no other methods which ean do this.
