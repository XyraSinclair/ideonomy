ATTITUDES AND REAL AND IDEAL THINGS
Pat Gunkel

There is a tendency to suppose that an abstract triangle (whatever that may be,
since eg it may be dynamic and complex mental processes expressed to a degree,
even in the best insight a man ever had) is what the mind is occupied with when a
triangular form in the real world is perceived. Yet concrete forms such as
concrete triangles may actually be mch more specialized and even much more
generalized ‘thingst. It is a truism that things are perceived as a function of
setting and position in the stream of consciousness and biases of the observer.

En fact a brain is continually in the midst of subjective perspectives as if it

were specifically these perspectives, and not real or seeming parts, that are the
brain's concern and, for practical purposes, the brain's means for seeing ‘things’.
An example might be a triangle sitting on the ground and confronted by a rabbit.

The typical occurrence of any such form (and it is typical occurrences wherein a
triangle has signifigance) might be with a particular type and proportion of
triangle with its large base down or continuous with the ground or foreground,

and certainly with what only approaches a rigidly and properly geometrie (much
less axiomatic?!) triangle and a 2-dimensional, nonsolid, and perhaps empty triangle
as opposed, say, to a nose or treetop. It may actually be advantageous for an
animal (or a mant) to customarily pereeive a triangle as basedown and without

high, complete, or any rotatory invariance (indeed, the rotated triangle may

more nearly suggest to the animal another form at another orientation). (Same)
rotatory invariance may actually bring unnecessary complexity into the seene or

act. Men have difficulty substituting perspectives, even if the adopted perspective
is arbitrary: reversing optical illusions, reading upside down, reversing thought
processes, re-examining a picture, &. It is possible the very frequency of things
or the complex associative frequency of things determines much of the way we look

at things and gives a certain arbitrariness to perspective (since alternative
associations of things and depths of association are potentially infinite, some
exclusion and some choice mst occur). It may be that animals differ in the length
and complexity of the evoked potential and in the tenacity with which the hippocampus
perpetuates interests, and hence came to differ in their quotient of self-dissatisfac~
tion and in the degree of their fascination with ‘types' and ‘degrees of perfection'
of invariants.

It is an interesting idea that memic associations build up in association cortices
so as to yield excitory confluences inducing invariances by having became, in
phylogeny, more strong, acutely or chronically, than the lower cortices or rest of
the brain in seizing the motivational centers of the brain and hence the center of
the animals attention, wherein its attitudes toward the real and idéal may be supposed
to form, with quantitative &r qualitative progress in recognizing dnd attending to
invariants. Phylogenesis of masterful two-way connections of the frontal cortex
with the hypothalams & and of very gradual ontogenesis of the frontal cortex (eg
Synaptogenesis), perhaps concomitant with sensory interposition before motor product,
could well have favored the teased emergence of excitory confluences inducing
