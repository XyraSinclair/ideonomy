VARIANT DEFINITIONS OF ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE

Equivalent to ''expert systems'': the transfer of the specialist
knowledge, skills, methods, style, insights, reasoning
processes or professional experience of human experts to
computer software.

Equivalent to "knowledge engineering'': the creation in computer
systems of an ability to creatively, usefully or logically
process and manipulate knowledge; or equivalent to "semantic
information processing''.

- Equivalent to "symbolic computation''!: the use of computers to

process symbolic, not just numerical, information.
Experimental epistemology: mechanical simulation and exploration

for scientific purposes of the fundamental laws, mechanisms
and possibilities of knowledge and its development.

Equivalent to ''pure and applied ideonomy'': the science and
technology of ideas and their laws.

. The farthest frontiers of computer software.

The farthest frontiers and possibilities of computer science and
engineering.

- The discipline that attempts to re-create, simulate or explain

the human mind, or brain and mind, in computer models or
analogues; equivalent to "cognitive science": the discipline
that would understand human thinking.
Equivalent to "universal noology'': the universal science of al]
possible (natural or artificial) minds or forms of intelligence.
Equivalent to ''nooengineering'': the branch of technology that
seeks to build minds.
The branch of technology that strives to create machines that are
intelligent or alive.
The attempt to mechanize or automate logic, or the science of
reasoning (in theory this need not be equivalent to any of the

above).
The attempt to give computers 'consciousness', self-awareness,
egohood, 'purpose' or 'free-will'.

The attempt to make computers self-programming, or to perfect
autoprogramming.

The effort to give computers the ability to learn, in general or
else unaided by man, or to mechanize induction.

The attempt to create computers that are self-developing or
self-evolving.

Equivalent to ''computer psychology'': the technology that would
confer not just intelligence but other psychological dimensions
on computers, and the complementary science that would
understand such possibilities theoretically.

The attempt to synthesize ‘artificial humanity! in computers,
or to give them 'moral character': ethics, conscience, kindness,
a sense of beauty, spirituality, goodness, wisdom, altruism,
idealism, civilization, creative aspiration, dignity, etc.

The attempt to create the illusion of intelligence in computers,
or to give them substitutes for true intelligence.

The development of all the possibilities that lie between human
intelligence and today's computers and computer programs,

The attempt to create forms of intelligence and 'brains' other than
those found in natural organisms or man.
