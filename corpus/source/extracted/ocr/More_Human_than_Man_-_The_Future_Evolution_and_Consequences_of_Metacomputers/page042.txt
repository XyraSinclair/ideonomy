FUTURE USES OF A.1. NATURAL-LANGUAGE SYSTEMS

. Computers able to converse with babies to teach them language and

concepts, and otherwise able to nurse, entertain and improve
infants without human assistance.

- Automatic translation between all languages without human editing

or perceptible delay, both for oral and written communication;
books in all languages virtually in the same universal tongue.

- All films, television programs, and other public productions in

the electronic media effectively available without human effort
in all languages (e.g., automatic conversion of the voices of a
film's actors into identical voices speaking another language) .

. Automatic transcription of human speech into typescript via A.I.

Automatic transcription of text into a meaningfully modulated
synthetic voice.

- Robotic conversationalists and conversational robots capable of

sustaining elaborate dialogs on any topic with needy or idle
people: to develop conversational or speaking skills, receive
instructions or give instructions, be assigned or report
research, provide informational or other telephonic and non-
telephonic services, guide teleshoppers through electronic
catalogs, report equipment status, help run a factory, be
consulted by management, etc.
Robotic teachers and other educational machines at school and home.
Conversible home appliances and tools and scientific instrumentation.
Home computers that can be communicated with in plain English.
Military weapons and weapons systems that can be spoken to and that
speak in turn.
Airplanes, cars, and other vehicles that can be driven or instructed
by voice.
Conversible robotic clerks, tellers, secretaries, draughtsmen,
cameras, books, laser discs, pocket calculators, etc.
"Expert systems" that can be consulted, be developed and operate
in natural language.
Computer-aided design (CAD) using natural-language A.|.
Partially automated processing and personalization of the news.
Robotic editors, rewriters and writers (including computers able
to write any text in any style using bare facts or instructions
gotten from man).
Natural-language computers obviating computer programmers and other
intermediaries and many computer languages.
