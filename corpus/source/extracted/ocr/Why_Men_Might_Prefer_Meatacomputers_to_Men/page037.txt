"Things That May Be Obviated By Metacomputers--And Why''

(2)

Manually operated machines and, more generally, man's use of his
hands to manipulate the objects in his environment.

Natural sense organs and senses as the basis of perception.

Communication via the human voice, facial expressions, bodily
gestures, and physical compresence.

Mathematics learned and performed by man.

Typewriters and other writing utensils. (Metacomputers wil]

instantly and flawlessiy record dictation in any desired form
and style.)

Editing and rewriting.
Asking other persons questions. (Metacomputers will at any

instant be able to answer any question better than could another
human being.)

Memorization and remembrance. (Metacomputers will increasingly
be what man relies on for these things--rather than native
memory, which is extraordinarily weak and unreliable, besides
requiring effort.)

Persisting, and human, works of art. (Metacomputers will create
great new works of art on the spur of the moment that are
uniquely suited to the individual who requires them. To
reexperience any particular work of art--or to store or
distribute it--will be pointless and absurd.)

Travel.
Space civilization.
Encyclopedias. (Metacomputers will give man access to knowledge

in a novel, dynamically infinite form--and recast it in a form
ideally suited to the needs of each particular individual at any
instant. Moreover, this knowledge will be imparted via
representational technology and methods that are practically
without analogy to those of the present day.)

Much of tragedy.

Services rendered by human beings to other human beings.

Other living things (the continued existence and evolution of the
rest of earth's bios).

Man (human existence)!
