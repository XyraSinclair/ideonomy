ENERGIC. Of or related to energy.

VACUAL. Of or involving those interspaces outside the atmospheres of planets and
stars in which there is partial vacuum, tenuous plasmas, phenomena only little
visible (to ordinary instrumentation), transparency to distant emitters, &c
but what for most purposes seems a total void. Outside all the planets of
our Solar System but within the supposed boundary of the Solar System, called
the heliopause, there is a weak but complex, dynamic, and important atmosphere
which has been dubbed the rest of the heliosphere or the heliosphere, period.
Similarly the Milky Way galaxy is enclosed in an even more tenuous--but stil]
perfectly important, complex, dynamic, &c--atmosphere. Intergalactic Space
is an atmosphere rarer still, extraordinarily important, but almost completely
unknown and undescribed. All these interspaces are examples of particular
vacua. The vacua are not homogeneous but rather contain all sorts of gradients,
neighborhoods, intermediate zones, &. In the absence of a common term | use
the singular, vacuum, and the plural, vacua. Capitalization of both could be
justified.

TERRILUNAR = GEOSELENIAN. Of the Earth and the Moon together. This term might
be used to refer to an intertransportational system, cislunar Space, Lagrangian
colonies, &rc.

PLUTOGRAPHY. A description of, or the process of describing itself, the qualitative
&r quantitative resources (mineral, energic, environmental, &rc) of, or their
distribution on or within, celestial bodies such as the planets or Sun, &r the
intermediate regions or so-called ''vacua''. A term of such generality seems
requiredby the absence of any synonym and the needs of description and reference.
There are obviously any number of ways of characterizing ‘wealth. One might,
for example, do it in the absolute by describing the distribution of any or
particular minerals, elements, chemicals, &¢ in &r out of lodes, or rich
deposits, so that one's description is, in effect, timeless and ungoverned by
any momentary priorities or even other considerations; per contra, one might
characterize the distribution, kind, quantity, &rce of resources supposed or
known to exist in a region in more or less familiar or contemporary terms of
specific needs of the economy now or in the future, of real profitability or
exploitability of a given resource (say in terms of relative accessibility or
treatments presupposed for extraction), of the specific needs of regions of
Space or opportunities related to foreseeable development of enterprise &r
civilization in Space, of intraspacial needs, of possibilities for in situ
manufacturing and exportation of intensely valuable product, &rc.

TELEPLUTOGRAPHY. The process, or its product (say map), of describing the wealth
of celestial bodies or regions from afar, particularly from loci above their
hard or liquid surfaces or outside their atmospheres, say by scanning or
pictomapping satellites or by interplanetary active or passive telescopy,
say by ground-based observatories on Earth. Anything which may contribute to
an idea or picture of wealth or resources--spectrum of surficial radioactivity,
geometric 2- or 3-dimensional morphography, ground truth investigations,
gravimetry, magnetography, planetological theory, guesswork, comparative
planetography, &rc--is a potential contributor to plutography, and everything
done from extraplanetary loci is, by definition, teleplutography.

TELEMINERALOGY. Mineralogical teleplutography.

MICROLABORATORY. A set of instruments operated by a robot or remote control
and used in Space to describe or monitor artificial or natural situations, say
aspects of the natural environment on planetaries. The information obtained
being telecommunicated to a distant station, or stored for recovery at a later
date or per a particular need. Such a probe might perform experiments of
any degree of complexity. The main idea is that such microlaboratories will
be highly multipurposive, or pursue many different investigations. There wil]
