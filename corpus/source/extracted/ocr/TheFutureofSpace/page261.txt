US, USSR, PRC, UN, "ESRO' (Europe's NASA), Japan, Eastern Europe, and Brazil
have all highly competitive programs and agencies by 2000, and many other
nations "ride'' on these (eg building but not launching their own satellites,
receiving and paying for returns from Earth Resource satellites, adding
members to international teams, in effect paying taxes to certain agencies,
adding experiments and tasks to various missions, &c). There are many races
in Space involving attempts by various parties to beat and outdo one another
and dynamic one-upmanship. This competition extends beyond geosatellites

to unmanned and manned Lunar expeditions and bases, prototypal Lagrangian
colonies at L4 and L5, continuous and sizable ''Skylabs'' orbiting Earth,
interplanetary expeditions, a base on Mars, a manned expedition to the
asteroids, planetary science probes to all 9 planets, and a few active
transplutonian probes.

Various intranational and transnational corporations very strongly participate
in the activities of the preceding parties (in a thoroughly international

and businesslike way), and 3 corporations actually have their own, largely
independent programs (building or purchasing, and launching, their own
satellites, ''Spacelabs'', and a manned Lunar expedition).

The world in this way budgets $120 B/y on Space by 2000.

Controlled, safe, ideal, simple, nonpolluting, cheap, easy-to-build, and
surprisingly efficient fusion reactors are developed during the 1990's and
play an important role in producing power for both developed and underdeveloped
nations by 2000, a role accentuated by a tendency of the remarkable reactors
to replace fission and fossil power and by production of power by fusion
growing at a phenomenal rate in 2000. Simultaneously, doubt and antagonism
existing in connexion with nuclear energy between 1970-85, spends itself

in the subsequent years, particularly as fusion power emerges and proves
abnormally safe and is affirmed by environmentalists as an ideal or at least
acceptable alternative to fission and chemical energy, which come to be seen
as both polluting and unsafe.

Industry proves that it need not pollute the environment or infringe on the
public welfare by what it does, and that previous damage to the environment
is reversible, and environmentalist forces strike more moderate and compromising
stances as decades pass and the value of growth once again becomes obvious
and an important human motivation. In fact, by 2000 the developed and
developing countries as one begin to seem very seemly places despite heavy
industry and sustained economic growth. This inconsistency is by itself
sufficient to revolutionize fair-minded and, indeed, popular attitudes toward
economic growth, technology, and Faustian goals, except in those blinded

by ideology, whose role in things however declines by 2000. Partly these
benign attitudes and reconciliations occur over the world because of a
stubborn refusal by almost all underdeveloped countries to abandon the ideal
of growth and of catching up with the--themselves progressive--more developed
nations.

The energy crisis of the 1970's fades away as alternative sources of energy
(coal, shale, solar, fission, fusion, &c) become competitive and important

in nations' affairs, OPEC disintegrates and compromises, and the atmosphere
of fear and uncertainty prevailing throughout the 1970's disappears.

The proliferation of nuclear weaponry among Nth countries, the exponential
and autocatalytic expansion of military forces in Space, the evolution of
baser defensive and offensive weaponry in Space, certain major military
confrontations between the Great Powers before 2000, the advancement of
various Spacial reconnaissance and surveillance systems and capabilities,

the inducement to a military role provided by the expansion and diversification

of the world's overall Spacial enterprise, &¢, cause tremendous growth of the
world's military investments in Space.
