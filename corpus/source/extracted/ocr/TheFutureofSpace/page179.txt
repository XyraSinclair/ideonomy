The year 2020 can serve as the time by which man should have in his possession x
a fair idea as to the wealth of his Solar System, the energies, materials, and
possibilities present in it. He should have arrived at such a preliminary but
important overview on the basis of theory combined with exploration. There
has been discussed in the preceding pages the development and use ot
almost fantastically powerful means for exploring the environments and mapping
the multifarious wealth of the planets, satellites, and asteroids, and the
evolution of an intricate picture of the Solar System as a whole. This has
been combined with suggestions as to the rate of exponential progress of
science, technology, economies, human purpose, and Spacial enterprise. By
2020 the 30 major planetaries should have been pictomapped at a topographic
resolution of square millimeters, at many octaves of the electromagnetic
Spectrum, with superscientific spectral analysis, in combination with pictures now
impossible of structure and composition 100 kilometers under their solid
surfaces, and in combination with equally extraordinary techniques too numerous
to mention. All of these planetaries will have been landed, or received
probes in their jovoid atmospheres and oceans, and examined in situ over perhaps
0.1%-1.0% of their surface. Tremendous empirical and theoretical development
of planetology will have occurred, and there will exist a paleoplanetology of
such stature as to be of great economic value in indicating the distribution
and exact location of elemental, chemical, and mineral deposits throughout the
Solar System. Even apart from these grand things, there will certainly have
been answered many simple and obvious questions about plutographic possibilities
of an absolutely decisive nature, such as the degree of analogy of other
planetaries to Earth, whether convenient fractionations exist within the
Solar System, the degree of variation over this territory in any case, the
ease of retrievability of at least the cream of the deposits, the complementarity
of resources on other planetaries to Earth and human needs, the roles of other
environments in the generation of valuable deposits, and the differentiation
or nondifferentiation of other planetaries. It will be a fair idea in part
because of the incentive it will supply to exploit the vast resources of the
Solar System, to develop Space as a whole, to take the rest of the Solar System
as pur home, and the glamour it will attach to Space. Parts of the picture
will exist well before 2020, even before 2000, and are even accessible today.
The assembly of such a picture is in a way the meaning of what man is doing in
Space even now. By 2020 there will have arisen as well, in the breast and brain
of mankind, a stupendous vision of the future of the Solar System, the very
thing which is lacking today. This will be the fair idea.
