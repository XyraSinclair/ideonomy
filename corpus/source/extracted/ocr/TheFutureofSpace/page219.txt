THROWAWAY vs CYCLIC. The first term refers to such parts or wholes of spaceships
or other objects as we might profitably throw away rather than burdening
ourselves with the necessity of their transportation to destination or their
return at nonlinear cost. "Cyclic!', in contradistinction, refers to an act
or practise of returning such things 3, I, or many times. Economies both
ways can be argued and matters have yet to be decided. For example, an SSTO
(Single Stage to TakeOff) vehicle might or might not be most economical &r
convenient for Earth-into-Space Flight. Relative economies may prove variable
according to different situations &r uses.

PIGGY-BACKING. The carrying of many spacecraft &r spacestations by single,
capacious spaceships in order to effect total flight and operational economies,
the contained or attached "piglets" being dispatched in various clusters
and orders at | or more "dispatch loci’. Put otherwise, large craft will
Piggy-back many craft, devices, materiels, and personnels to sets or systems

SUBSATELLITE. Today this term is used by the space community to refer to a
probe and a set of probes carried by a spaceship to another planet and released
upon arrival to effect seperate landings, assume different buoyant positions
in the atmosphere, &r assume various orbits.

SATELLOID. A satellite able to maintain itself in an orbit so low as to ordinarily
be unstable or impossible, compensating atmospheric resistance by continuous
or periodic propulsion, thereby filling the very large gap between ordinary
astronautical and aeronautical flight and actually allowing very low orbits
with little expenditure of energy per instant or per eternity. Unlike
ground-based aircraft, such a satelloid might actually return to Space and
be space-based. It is interesting to consider what tasks might be fulfilled
by future satelloids operating in the atmosphere of Earth and other planets.
Such low-propulsional satelloids might maintain themselves in perpetual orbit,
if not by intervehicular refueling, by "solelectric'! drives, exomotive ablation
Or conversion using continuous or periodic ground-based or space-based lasers,
"aerofuel" fusion, 6&rc. Clearly the configuration of satelloids would be
appropriately aerodynamic.

SOLELECTRIC. Of or relating to power or propulsion converting captured sunlight
into electrical energy.

AEROFUEL. Of or relating to fuel taken from the surrounding atmosphere by
aeronautical &r astronautical ramjets, satelloids, or atmosphere-skimming craft;
such fuel itself.

HOLOVISION. Holographic television. Between 1975-2000 various methods will be
developed and implemented by networks for stereoscopic (binocular), holographic,
and "ultraholographic" television and videophony. Difficult problems will be
presented by the need to replace preexistent networks, but not insuperable
ones owing to the simplicity of possible converters using identical receptions.
By ultraholographic television is meant the large number of improvements over
simple holovision which are foreseeable, including cycloramic holovision and
of such kind--what might be called "'spherovision''=-that viewing angle will
alter so rapidly with movement of the head that scenes viewed will readily be
rotated through 360° by active observers. Indeed, viewers might be equipped
with sufficient powers that scenes will be fixed unless a viewer chooses to
move around by rotating, selecting or altering the distance of parts of, altering
the perspective of, &rc a scene, &r to otherwise alter and select what he senses.
All these things will probably presuppose much greater instantaneous transmission
of information, regardless of the routes taken by such information or the
distant location of various telecommunicators. Telecommunication via geosatellitic
relays should grow accordingly, by many orders of magnitude, as such sophisticated
and heavy-informational systems are implemented.
