GLOSSARY OF SPECIAL TERMS USED IN THIS BOOK

PLANETARY. A planet or smaller but analogous celestial body such as a moon or
an asteroid; the term is thus useful in being more general than ''planet'’.

TELESCOPY. Use of telescopes, techniques therein, &r the telescopes themselves.

TRANSPLUTONIAN. More distant from the Sun than the orbit of the planet Pluto.

GRAND IGLOOS. Future epiplanetary or extraplanetary constructions making use
of ices, waters, and analogous substances present in abundance and sometimes
conveniently at loci in the Solar System, with the common property of having low
melting points and sufficiently higher boiling points as to be used in
construction when at liquid temperatures (perhaps in the vicinity of room
temperatures) and to solidify as the desired materials and structures a
short time later or naturally owing to the cryogenic conditions found in
Space; some whole moons, for example, may be largely ice; the point is that
these ices will be easily used and yet combine properties such as glass-like
transparency with exploitable crystallinity, &c. Massive water will only
naturally be in transport across the Solar System in times to come so
the marriage is one of very great convenience.

GLACIOTECHNOLOGY. The science, technology, and engineering which will arise in
the future for construction purposes (such as the building of "grand igloos'',
titanic mirrors or lenses of ice, &c) and other industrial purposes (such
as applications of cryogenic ice in electronic and photonic circuitry) using
the immense quantities of icy materials which must surely exist throughout
the Solar System.

SPACE HYDROLOGICAL AUTHORITY (SHA). A hypothetical intranational, international,
supranational, or independent authority an equivalent of which will probably
arise in the 2lst century in Space and play an important role in the investigation,
discovery, promotion, coordination, overseeing, mapping, quantitation, regulation,
apportionment, conservation, development of technology and engineering
appropriate to, development, provision, &r perhaps directly in the use of
the myriad, finite, idiosyncratic, obscure, variously recoverable, &c water
resources of the total Solar System of such importance to co-emergent
Solian Civilization during its early years and later.

SUPERMANEUVERABILITY. This will refer to highly or totally maneuverable orbital
Er nonorbital probes, stations, and craft possessed of, eventually fairly
extraordinary, powers. Such powers may and will include the ability to change
to any latitude, longitude, altitude, velocity (including 0), orbit (eg
synchronous orbit), &¢, to do so reversibly (including takeoff and landing),
to do so 1-100 times or eternally, to assume any eccentricity of orbit,
amphibious or omniphibious abilities, powers to execute complicated maneuvers,
to return to Earth (eg for refueling, removal of film or tapes, sample, crew,
&c, repair, reequipping, or redesign), for any or all of the planetaries or
beyond the Solar System, &¢. Thus economies will be realized over many and
diverse serial and parallel devices.

AMPHIBIOUS and OMNIPHIBIOUS. Amphibious means that a spaceship will combine
capabilities for operating and passing between 2 quite distinct media, such
as the surface of a planet and space, possibly in either or both directions.
Omniphibious refers to greater versatility still such as seems called for
in order for there to be craft which will be able to pass from space into
ocean &r atmosphere, perhaps in any order, and freely. Customary transportational
systems that will arise for Space eventually may, of course, achieve greatest
systemic efficiency by seperating and linking operational and vehicular stages
in multistepped and polylocal transport, rather by unification of operations,
vehicles, and systems; so far in Space, Americans have tended toward multistepping
and the Russians toward unification, and it is hard to predict any denouement
of this competition of practises.

VERY-MOONED PLANETS. Those planets--the jovians, except Neptune--possessing
more than 2, in fact more than 4, moons (Jupiter 213, Saturn 210, Uranus 235).
