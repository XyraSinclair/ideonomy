The prevalent if unconscious assumption is that a simple inverse relationship
exists for the quality of any populations in quantity greater than our own.

In truth, no such law, possessed of any directive value and sign, is known

to correlate population quantity and quality, except insofar as the sign is
generally positive, per supra. There are locations over the world in which
Population sizes and densities accompany seemingly inferior conditions of
life relative to some implicit scalar or vectorial optimum; there are equally
locations where such relationships exist for superior conditions. Often

the attribution of inferior conditions to dense or large populations, as

the effect of these abundant Populations, is laughable, an example being
where the standards of living in great American cities have fallen simply

as the effect of the self-governing rural or suburban migration of the higher
socioeconomic categories, so that the lower and more objectionable categories
have become concentrated in these great cities and indeed disfigured the
urban environment. Great European cities have hitherto been immune to the
tragic plight of the American cities. Even now, in the case of a great city
like New York experiencing all the problems it is, native Yorkers are devoted
to their city and spurn the countryside as dull, empty, strange, and inconvenient.
In a city like Boston, despite its average problems, a dense section like

the Italian North End has a color and safety very attractive to new residents.
In short, it as easy to defend as to criticize the life of large and dense
populations, and the critical question is the net situation and as enjoyed

by optimistic citizens, rather than those Projecting distaste and rejection
onto surprisingly neutral circumstances. To a critical degree, it can be
said that one gets out of life what he puts into it in terms of constructive,
affirmative, relaxed, and cheerful behavior: the social environment is the
projected product of the social mind. There was a day when the immensity

of Terran population was a source of delight, pride, and hope. The current
alternating weltschmerz should die with the age.

Enlargement of population has the fantastic consequence that it, in effect,
compresses time, accelerates Progress and development, and brings the future
into the present. Of course there is some Progress which is too serial and
dependent upon nondemographic factors that it can be hurried by the trick of
expanding the population, variety, wealth, and complexity of the present.

Yet even against this must be placed the nonlinear advantages of such expansions,
