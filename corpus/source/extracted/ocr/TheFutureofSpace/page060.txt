A literal or figurative break-even point for aggregate or sectoral spacial industry,
representing positive net profit after debts paid for a typical year or over
the entire past of man's investment in space, for the United States or the
entire world, might as well be reached around 1990. Admittedly this prophecy is
truly oracular. What force it does have expresses the explicit or implicit belief
that by about 1990 man's involvement in space will be a paying proposition and
not an absolute expense to society, as it hypothetically is today. Arguments have
been made that space has long since paid for itself and in fact will profit the
United States and Earth several billion dollars per annum until the end of time
as a result of technology already implemented, without even taking into
consideration temporary or lasting benefits of billions of dollars produced by
all sorts of obvious and subtle spin-off. Of course, estimation of net profit
or loss may also be done in other terms such as some idea of social needs and
opportunities unrelated to space but suitable for such investment, charity,
interest, or development, or some period of amortization. Or the criterion
for a break-even point might be expressed in terms of the day when intraspacial
life and industry will be economically self-sufficient or independent, or when
the balance of trade will favor Space. It might be expressed either for public
or private enterprise, or for either industry on Earth or the origin, growth,
and proportionateness of wares, information, &r materials produced in space and
visibly returned to Earth. In any case, the period before the year 2000 is likely
to be the epoch over which man's interest in space justified itself in the net
in purely economic terms and ordinary cash flow, and in terms sensible even to
statesmen, and so to constitute either a causal or acausal turning point, at least
for some.
