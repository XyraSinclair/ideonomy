Actually man has sufficient resources that he could build many large ''colonies''
at every one of the loci just listed starting tomorrow and concurrently. What

is not realized is that the human race could only gain, overall, from undertaking
these many purposes, even in the face of immediate failure such as is certainly
possible. Such failure could simply result from insufficient resolve, wrong
administration or planning, wrong people being involved, niggardliness about
funds and style, premature pessimism, &rc. Similarly, man can and, should now
undertake the complete planning and building of whole cities of 10 inhabitants,
but with the same necessary ingredients just underlined. We have to learn to

do these things and how to do them right and perfectly, and these things presume
experience, even a taste of outright failure. In this way we can build the
better environments that we all crave as opposed to the accidental environments
accreted over the past and suffered by ourselves today. Colonies in Space

are a special challenge, and a particularly desirable one, merely because the
environments of Space are especially difficult to colonize because of the

Strange conditions, unknown resources, seeming emptiness, inhuman difficulties,
&c. In every imaginable respect we will have to rely on human ingenuity and

we will have to acquire a profound understanding of natural and artificial things,
of what makes a society, city, environment, world, civilization, finer human
being, good system of values, &¢. These are the things which are important for
what human beings do with themselves in this universe, not lesser things or
compromises!

Such a level of mastery in understanding, controlling, and remaking the human
world has an importance illustrated by the fact of what man has previously
done to nature. He has found§@ himself in Eden, a natural paradise in terms
of weather, biology, landscape, resources, and his tribal beginnings. He has
then progressed by exploiting and desecrating these accidents of nature, by
taking them for granted, by slowly degrading things, &c. What he must do in
the future if his destiny is not the contrivance of Hell amid a ruined environment
is learn to understand the character and springs of natural beauty and resources
and the techniques for preserving, extending, transcending, and re-creating ex nihilo
all that previously was found wild and abundant in Nature. He must learn how
to play a responsible God. What wil] happen if he fails to do so, if he somehow
lacks the will or impedes himself, is all too clear from the course of the world
over the past 500 years. Naturally it might be possible to first destroy the
world and then set about achieving things, but this seems an unnecessary and a
dangerous course. It may happen that in 50-200 years man wil] successfully so
wreck or poison his fortunate home that he will be compelled to migrate to Space
and begin things from scratch, perhaps in a post-holocaustic world with the
belligerents mutually annihilated and the survivors mere bystanders at scattered
and chance outposts. Such a post-war emigration might occur as the aftermath
of a relatively mild catastrophe, with the emigrants but a fraction of the survivors
and represented by the wiser, indignant, more farseeing, more or less stricken,
more tasteful, &rc few; the human race might even divide in this way, with the
peaceful or the bellicose abandoning the disfigured Earth for raw Space.

By 2000-2050 the Earth may be so affluent that, paradoxically, a reversion to
natural, cruder, leaner, harsher, alien, &re conditions marking early life in
Space may be less resisted by men than today when our transition from such original
conditions is too early and greater intolerance for such conditions describes
mankind. Life in these later years may be so easy as to give rise to widespread
dissatisfaction and some eagerness for more Strenuous experiences. Men may simply
have become naive about such difficulties and hence overready to embrace them.
