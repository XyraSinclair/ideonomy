That the human race is entering an age free of the need to work in the usual
sense and yet possessed of the means to transform its experience entirely
means that an opportunity is before it to pick and build its world according
to artistic standards of perfection. This would seem to be the opportunity
afforded by Space where we have the option of creating worlds ex nihilo or
ex capite, motivated by the wish to perfect human existence. The life of the
farmer is, in a way, the ideal life because of the farmer's relationship to
the land, to time, to himself, and to his crops. A similar and a consecutive
synthesis would be provided by the task of building colonies in the void of
Space by the convergent demands it would put upon heart, brain, and muscle
in the creation of one's own world. The world would accumulate about one as
the art of past moments and other lives. Such self-creating and self-perfecting
worlds would be a pleasant fate for men relieved of the need to work but thereby
set adrift. Artistic endeavor permits the endless adventure of the search for
an unattainable perfection through the infinite exploitation of past accomplishment.
An argument against the colonization of Space which is often raised is that
such colonization, if desirable and feasible, might better or just as wel] be
done, at least in the beginning or until necessity demands relocation, in various
clement or hostile environments on Earth--on, in, or under the sea, or, in, or
under the polar ice, on or under deserts or jungles, in or atop mountains, under
the ground anywhere in the world, or even (what is possible in terms of floating
cities which have already been sketched by R. Buckminster Fuller) in the atmosphere.
A fit reply is an affirmation of the desirability and feasibility of colonizing all
these spaces more or less simultaneously. Such choices would vary human habitats
and maximally stimulate science, technology, engineering, and human nature. These
are challenges which we really should not pass up. We have done so hitherto
for false reasons such as the added cost and difficulty of such undertakings,
