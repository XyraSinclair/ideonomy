Why will people, even hypothetically, wish to visit or live in Space? After all,
the Earth is a natural and cultural paradise, and will at first represent everyone's
home.

One reason for touristry to geocentric Space is the panoramic view it wil]
provide of so-called Spaceship Earth below, behind, outside, and above. It is
unlikely that still and motion pictures, paintings, narratives, and stories wil]
quite capture the quality, thrill, personal experience, and accomplishment of
direct flight into Space. The excitement of anticipating and undertaking the
flight itself (the ascent, orbit, and descent) will surely be enormous and unique.
The enthusiasm afterwards of those returned to Earth will be highly contagious.
There will be prestige and Veblenesque mimicry at work. By the years referred to
mankind will differ considerably from ourselves in 1975. Families in the wealthiest
countries will be many times richer than now. By then many current thrills will
be old hat and men will be looking elsewhere for fun and adventure. Great momentum
will have gathered about Space. Current hesitations, uncertainties, fears, and
criteria of what is normal will have died. Spacial enterprise will have grown
enormous ly--disproportionately even to economic growth and to technical means for
Spacial ventures. Commercial interest in Space will have opened up and explored
all possibilities. Traffic between Earth and Space of robots, freight, and employees
will have developed apart from any touristry but thereby have supplied the basis
