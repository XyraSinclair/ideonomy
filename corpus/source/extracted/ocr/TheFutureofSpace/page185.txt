astronomic observations possible only at such unlikely depths (such observations

Even if depositional and differentiational stratification in the cooler and
younger interiors of the minor planetaries such as the Moon is to be supposed
much less, and thus much less interesting, than in the case of the Earth, the
mineralogy and mining possible at depths in these planetaries holds much
attraction from the standpoint of future human industry, even as it will have
evolved by 2025. Moreover, in addition to vertical stratification in this simple
sense, some sort of nesting or even hierarchical clustering (or miscellany of
lodes) may be entertained as existing within all planetaries, hot or cold,
of tremendous and even comparable value to future mineral-hungry men. Certainly
the best way to discover quantitative and qualitative characteristics of planetary
interiors might be to go there directly, at least on one occasion or at intermediate
and indicative depth. Conceivably a shaft might be artificially walled and
buttressed, particularly as civilization grows or ''megamachines'' are built and
used; walls might even be artificially fused and thus frozen. Just conceivably,
or by 2100, continuous terrawatt basers, or the like, might be used to penetrate
planetaries at immense depths. The more consolidated materials at great depths
might resist nuclear compaction; but smal] planetaries might be less consolidated.
Certainly an asteroid like Ceres or smaller could represent a site appropriate
