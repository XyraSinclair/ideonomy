A new school of physics, called ''plenistic'', will arise over the next 25-100
years. Nature will variously be found to be of unsuspectedly great, or of
infinite, subdivision, structure, density, energy, dimensionality, complexity,
hierarchy, variability, &rc. The experiential universe will come to
be considered as in some sense a ''plenum'', for example of infinitely dense
hierarchical structure, constituted of an infinity or more (per transfinite
arithmetic or developments in set theory, analysis, topology, &c) of particles
and forces, having infinite dimensionality, &r the like. Possibilities here
are themselves infinite, but some of those now living who have had ideas of
this unconventional kind are P.A.M. Dirac, John Archibald Wheeler, Sir Fred
Hoyle, Gerald Feinberg, H.C. Dudley, Geoffrey Chew, Pat Gunkel, &c. In fact
the debate is ancient in its roots, an immemorial philosophic issue. Modern
physical understanding merely perpetuates the plenistic view in one of its
variants. It may be that all but 1% of the mass of the known universe is
invisible, existing as neutrinos or black holes, that ''real'' energy and mass
are infinitesimal alongside the energy density of the so-called vacuum, that
the material and energic density of any space-time point is infinite or
"superinfinite'’ owing, inter alia, to the bidirectionally infinite frequency
and energy spectra of nature, &c. Such physics, as it presumably emerges in
its greater glory, will lead to progressive scientific discovery and applications
in terms of usable resources, transportation, communication, exploration,
inventions, &rc. Returning to the nature of such a physics, some groups
of possibilities are that nature is literally full of infinitely and
infinitesimally graded residual types of relationships, that these relationships
continuously or discontinuously grade off from one another (the latter includes
the possibility that some natura! qualities and realms have only an indirect,
or possibly purely asymmetric, relationship to or continuity with each other),
that the universe is of infinite physical extent (in terms of space, time,
microscopy of both, qualitative relation, organization and complexity, novelty,
laws, &c), that order and disorder (or symmetry and asymmetry) simultaneously
always increase, that cosmogony is continuous and possibly also omnipresent,
that an infinity of infinitely different ''parallel'' worlds coexist, that both
“determinism! and ''indeterminism'' are far greater and different things than
supposed in past discussion, and so on. If such uncustomary circumstances
in fact prevail, then they will inevitably open the gate to the greatest
adventure man has ever known in terms of both extensive and intensive exploration
of the universe that embodies him and his order. It is conceivable that
civilizations elsewhere in the Milky Way and Universe give no sign of themselves
for the simple reason that scientific and technological development invariably
and virtually directly leads to the abandonment of the conquest of outer space,
as perceived by man, to the pursuit instead of such strange realms as are here
contemplated. In any case, the figurative or elementary exploration of such
plenistic realms will commence with certain investigations in 2000 and forward.
it is almost impossible to predict the consequences of such ongoing
investigations and discoveries for the future of Space, but ,
they will doubtless be radical-and critical.
