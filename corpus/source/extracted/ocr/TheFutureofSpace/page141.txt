The year 2013 can stand for |the year by which an exodus of men departing Earth
for Space will have begun. It will be a touristic and semiresidential exodus
more or less at once and reciprocally: the curves of annual growth of tourists
and residents will at this time and until 2100 be very complex in their mutual
relationships and are difficult, for this reason, to foresee. The situation
partly derives its difficulty from the facts that tourists will become residents
and residents tourists, permanency will be ambiguous and highly varied, men
will have a conflicting multitude of purposes in Space, qualitatively different
Spacial loci will be opening up at uncertain moments and rates, repeated and
"“dialectical'' waves are to be anticipated (smoothable to an uncertain degree),
almost organic nonlinearities are to be expected, patterns of development wil]
be so extraordinarily contingent upon "'catalytic'' events, &c. The upward inflection
expected in 2013, however, will in part embody an autocatalytic phenomenon. It
is the turbulent nature of the explosive development of Space that is so forbidding
for any modeling and extrapolation here: it renders equiprobable and highly improbable
the most diverse set of long-term alternatives so that Space over decades and
centuries will be curiously free and pluripotent. Over time, especially at this
time forward, tourists will be penetrating ever deeper into the Solar System
and venturing to the outer planets. If unique, and not just narrowly uniform,
voyages are possible for tourists, perhaps as a function of what they are willing
to pay for fuel and time, then a wide and diverse spectrum of trip distances and
types can be anticipated, and spaceships might even be more or less for rent or
even individual purchase by groups of suitable size or persons of adequate wealth.
Such innovative adventure could conceivably be an interesting phenomenon with
important consequences between 2013-2060, with an important bearing on the overall
development of the Solar System. Critical considerations are falling costs and
rising income over this multigenerational and distant period, as well as positive
touristic feedbacks apt to give a new face to things and accelerate change.
