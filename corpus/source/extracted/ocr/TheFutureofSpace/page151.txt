Perhaps by 2015 engines or velocities of 1,000,000 mph, which is 1,500,000

feet a second or 1/700th the speed of light, will have been achieved or come

into customary use for certain purposes, or be under development for such

standard or more widespread use. What is the point of such speed? It might

be necessary for flight to Pluto in 3 months, or for commuting between

Earth and Mars in a few weeks or days (one way). Theoretically one could travel

to the Moon from the Earth in a few minutes or less than an hour. Gorgeous

waste of energy on acceleration might be unnecessary if part of the energy

could be recaptured by landing energy regeneration systems (systems using

terminal satellites and functioning like the energy levels of atoms, have been
suggested between Earth and the Moon). Flight at 1,000,000 mph, being 10

times faster than at 100,000 mph, could be justified merely on the basis of

human wage savings or the duty cycle with a tenfold use (which might apply to

even higher velocities, in which case early traffic could be handled by a

very few ships, or the traffic could be very enlarged). The means of propulsion
for such rapid intrasolian flight might be various nuclear systems possessed of various
accelerations, or exomotive propulsion by planetofugal or interlocal basers.
Theoretically, at least, the velocity and accéleration of interplanetary ships
using solar energy could be increased merely by increasing their collector's

area, providing the weight would stay efficient and the strain tolerable. A
critical question is what acceleration is possible, tolerable, and efficient

at points in the future for man, automata, and simple projectile. Conversion
schemes would have to be possible, say for exomotive propulsion by ground-based
basers or by interplanetary heliocentric arrays. Machines might tolerate

many orders of magnitude greater acceleration (g-force) than men. What would

be the maximal acceleration possible for automata or mere projectiles (''bullets'')?
On the other hand, future science and technology may enable men to withstand

far greater accelerations for far greater periods (or perhaps indefinitely),

and narrow the man-machine or man-bullet gap. Epiplanetary &r extraplanetary
linear accelerators of tremendous power &r length might launch men, machines,

or bullets at incredible accelerations and velocities. Such accelerators

might eventually be 100-9 miles long, or circular, even wrapped around planetaries;
they might even be helical. Some accelerators might be ''resonators'', machines
accelerating objects by vibrating them elastically between 2 equidistant poles
(say 2 flelds of force). Various explosive accelerators (''guns'') are imaginable
that would use electromagnetic energy, electrostatic energy, chemical energy,
stored and released pressure, nuclear energy or explosion, &r the like to launch

a projectile from a tube ("barrel'') or ramp. Assuming maximal acceleration
tolerable to ship ér load, barrels could be constructed 1-1000 miles deep in
planetaries at the bottom of which instantaneous or sustained nuclear, chemical,
rc explosions could occur, the released pressure pushing the unhappy projectile

up and out of the inexpansible barrel at astronomic speeds, perhaps conveying

the projectile to Pluto or out of the Solar System and to adjacent stars; such
modes of launch might actually use energy with high efficiency (ballistic systems
may, however, suffer from ceilings on efficiency at maxima). What velocity and
efficiency maxima would apply to so-called ''mass-drivers'', perhaps competitively?
No one has so far indicated any optimal maximum of velocity for future intrasolian
operations, or the suprema in terms of efficiency or cost: a critical fact. An
interesting question is what rates of transportation will be tried and used on
Earth for nonspacial purposes, at points in time. Sufficient velocities or
accelerations may render types of ''teleportation'' more efficient or cheaper than
the transportation of objects &r men, favor the transmission of information 6&r
power, or curtail such things altogether, at least at distinct points in the future.
Since escape velocity from the Solar System is 138,000 mph, the speeds under
discussion could be the basis of interstellar flight: Alpha Centauri (a system of the 3
_closest stars) could be reached, at 106 mph, in 3 millennia, by 5000 AD.
