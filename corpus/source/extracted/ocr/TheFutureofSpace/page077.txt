Mars, a relatively far more hospitable resource than the Moon, will fairly bloom
in the spring between 1997 and 2045. It will be explored, populated, and
developed at an almost hectic pace over 50 years, and will rapidly become, to
all intents and purposes, self-supporting. Life there will have a romantic,
ensorcelling aspect, and visitors will stay, becoming Martians. Backpack and
vehicular compressors and converters will enable men to breath the thin air,

3% the density of Earth's. Considerable water may be found in extensive permafrost.
Enormous domes and tents may be erected over great swatches of soil, pressurized
with compressed and converted air, irrigated with recovered water, and heated

by ingenious use of greenhouse effects imprisoning the natural heat of the Sun
received over the identical acreage, the resulting ecosystem enabling
low-technologic silviculture, agriculture, floriculture, and human inhabitation
of Edenic image transfigured by the Martian adventure and the dreams of men.

A hundred asteroids in circular and eccentric orbits will have been individually
visited by man or machine by 1997, and 10,000 by 2010, only 13 years later. It
is even conceivable that one or more probes, or a team or force of men, will be
created for the express purpose of the continuing investigation of the asteroids,
especially the asteroidal belts, in a systematic and exhaustive way. Although
the collective mass of the asteroids is much less than the mass of the Earth,
their collective surface area is enormous and presents quite a challenge, as
well as opportunity, to explorers and developers. The Asteroidal Survey, as
it may officially be called, will have as its object the cataloging, photographing,
morphological and mineralogical mapping (and possibly the pictomapping) ,
classification, and early experimental development (mining, occupation,
industrialization, &c) of the 100,000s of large and small asteroids that exist.

The fragmentation and possible mineral fractionation of the asteroids may make
them ideal for mining and conceivably an Eldorado, in which case the Survey
will have its hands full and the tide of Solian civilization will advance by
1-3 AU. The automata usable by the Survey in the early decades of the 21st
century will, of course, be fantastically sophisticated both in terms of their
analytic methods and instrumentation and sheerly in terms of their perceptual
and conceptual power, thus accelerating, perfecting, and enlarging the survey
that will be made. What early use will be made of the asteroids is not clear
at this point, but is obviously critical. They could, for example, seed
massive occupation and become an archipelago of "kingdoms".
