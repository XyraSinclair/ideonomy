Whence the power, tools, raw materials, processed materials, chemicals,
manufactures, services, &c for the colonies, initially and later?
Whence the fuel, air, food, construction materials, machines, implements,
&c? To what extent, and when, will these come from Earth, the native,
or another environment? What safety margins are appropriate for such
estimates in terms of actual planning? What tradeoffs between raw
materials, elements, chemicals, minerals, tools, comforts, &c are possible
and probable? What would these tradeoffs mean?

What kind of people could and should go into Space, or to these colonies,
say developmentally?

Would such colonies be endangered by ransomers, madmen, terrorists, enemies, kids,
or simply by fallible men?

What materials, manufactures, machines, energies, supplies (initial, developmental,
and enduring), innovations, researched productions, &¢ will colonies require
--types, amounts, standards, priorities, forms, rates, &c¢?

. What ''size laws'', or other engineering laws, configure any colonies--and how?

. What don't we know, what do we need to know, how do we find it out, &c?

. Might colonies anywhere in Space be easier or better, say initially, than

colonies under the sea, in Antartica, or elsewhere?

Can a case be made against the importance of initial or enduring transportation
costs?

Is the greatest, and a sufficient, warrant for colonization of a romantic,
heroic, spiritual, idealistic, psychological, philosophical, &rc nature?

. How fast could colonization develop over the next century (2000-2100)?
. What continued or semi-continued exponential progress (demographic, economic, global,

technologic, scientific, sociopolitical, plutographic, &rc) might be expected
such that it will cause or facilitate colonization?

Might there be some great--perhaps unsuspected, unpredictable, incomprehensible,
&r unmeasurable--aesthetic significance to life in a colony off somewhere
in Space or situated at particular prospects and settings?

Alternative, and rankable, power sources for such colonies: fission, chemical,
solar (eg photovoltaic vs optical, vs turboelectric), future fusion, &¢
in terms of specific and detailed proposals.

Actual patterns of fabricating and assembling colonies in situ.

Specific matters, problems, and opportunities of: air pressure, air composition,
artificial gravity, coriolis force, cosmic rays, ultraviolet light,
micrometeoroidal and macrometeoroidal bombardment, catastrophic rupture
threats, daily and seasonal cycles, inadvertent weather, wear and failure
of structures and materials, ecosystemic equilibria, tidal stresses and
strains, lifetime considerations, insulation requirements, problems of
excessive heat and cold, vacuum leakage, possible disasters or annoyances
(seisms, structural noises, barometric or gravitational discomforts, collisions,
effects of perpetual or nonterran day, light of strange composition and
distribution, possible crowding, &¢), choice of biota, utility of previously
developed and perhaps insistent technology and resources, recycling means
and limits, intracolonial transportation systems (foot, car, scheduled
public "'trains'", slidewalks, airplanes and balloons, subways, &c), &c.

The value, rate, kind, &¢ of all types and degrees of probable and possible
spin-offs to Earth or mankind from colonies.

The or any transitions from colonies to planeturgy and the exploration, occupation,
development, and transformation of whole planetaries; perhaps a transition
from a "'central'' (colonial) phase to a "pluralistic'' (homesteading) phase.

What is the psychological and moral significance of a colony, including such
things as defiance of the unknown, new life from responding to an overwhelming
physical challenge, filling the void with human meaning, development of
courageous and imaginative character, self-reliance, cooperation, &c?
