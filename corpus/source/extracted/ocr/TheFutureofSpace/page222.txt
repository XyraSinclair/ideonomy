SUBSPACIAL. Of or relating to that region below Space associated with a celestial
body, especially the Earth, say a suborbital trajectory of a projectile.

INTERLOCAL. Between explicit or implicit places or loci in Space or anywhere, or
with regard to this relationship. One could refer, for example, to interlocal
flight of a spaceship between Mars (one locus) and Earth (the other). The term
is a very useful generalization without a synonym,

MEGENNIUM, GIGENNIUM, &c. These are units of years specified by a known prefix
in the manner of "millennium. A megennium is a period of 1000 millennia or
of a million years, a gigennium is a period of a billion years (what geology
refers to as an eon), and so forth. These give rise to such natural derivatives
as megennia, megennial, &c. In the absence of equivalent or familiar units,
such words are very useful and should be put into universal use.

NONTERRAN. Of or relating to that other than, unlike, off, apart from, &rc the
Earth or Terran things. For example, one might refer to nonterran loci, people,
conditions, economies, needs, biologies, resources, affairs, &rc.

TERROID. Of or relating’ that having more or less the form or model of the Earth,
particularly in contradistinction to things jovoid. The 2 terms may be used
with reference to climate, atmospheric composition, planetary composition or
history, life, planetary size or Structure, &rc.

JOVOID. Of or relating to that having more or less of the character--as above--
of the jovian planets (Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune) as opposed to
those planets comparable to Earth (Earth itself, Mars, Mercury, Venus, &c).

These 2 terms--jovoid and terroid--are proposed out of the necessity for avoiding
confusion with specific references to the Earth, Jupiter, and the jovian planets,
and to limit the degree of analogy.

INTRACOLONIAL. Of or relating to that included within the walls or boundaries of
a Spacial colony, or to the internal affairs of such a colony, whether epiplanetary
or extraplanetary.

COLONIAL. Of or relating to a Spacial colony.

COLONY. A community of men in Space in extraplanetary Space or on another world,

&r the structure or structures (and other supplies, devices, &c) supporting a
community &r the region occupied (continuously or discontinuously) or possessed,
managed, used, &rc by a community. Such a community may be autonomous or
heteronomous, of any size, variously self-sufficient, &¢. The word "colony'!
connotes a community, population, &r structure larger, more serious, more
permanent, more diverse, &rc than a base, and yet smaller, earlier, less permanent,
less important, &érc than a city, and a fortiori a "planetary civilization".

PLANETARY CIVILIZATION. A civilization--population, culture, industry, ecology,
history, government, landscape, &rc--literally or figuratively covering a planetary,
perhaps having developed out of bases, colonies, &r cities as a result of immigration
&r native birth. Planetaries--because of area, environment, resources, location,
use, &rc--may differ in the sizes of population they support.

PROSOLUNAR. Of or relating to the nearside or earthward side of the Moon, being the
antonym of rerelunar.

PHOTOPONICS. The growing of plants, or other Stationary biological systems, in a medium
permeated with a luminous (quasi-insolative) matrix so as to benefit the cultures
by the effects of superabundant quasi-natural light, at least hypothetically; an
assumption of such a proposal might be that organisms are so opaque that they
generally fail to benefit from light having to penetrate far into their depths,
and yet that this superficial high-energy illumination may be critical for general
photosynthetic processes and productivity levels of light-dependent organisms.
Such internal illumination could be the basis for tridimensional or compact
bioculture (or ''stereoculture'').

PLANETOSPHERE. All the spheres--solid, liquid, gaseous, plasmic, electromagnetic,
gravitational, and any other--that contain and define a given planetary; thus
a planetary in its entirety, or a planetary at its extreme.
