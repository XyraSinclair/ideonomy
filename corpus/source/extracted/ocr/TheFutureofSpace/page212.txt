When the book is finished it will include detailed descriptions of 8 quarter
centuries from 1976 to 2175. So far about 2 quarters exist as first drafts.

A more complete and self-justifying picture, or story of what may come, will
therefore exist later. Many readers probably make the mistake of referring

all events described in the neighborhood of an initial date to that date

or to the contextual quarter. Actually the initial dates and events are
intended to stand for the first probable incidence of anything at all like

the topical event, however rudimentary or inanalogous, and the subsequent

very extensive and intricate discussion of events and dates jis intended to exhaust
related possibilities, voyage to future ages, include things both probable

and merely possible, hint the developments subsequent to and continuing the
initial event, &c. Some of the initial events are considered improbable,

and generally so characterized, and a few are only cited to be argued against
and eliminated. Moreover, much is present in the manuscript by the exercise
of an abnormal tolerance which | have always considered appropriate when
considering the larger possibilities of the world and the ideas of others;

it is difficult for any critic to be absolutely certain, and often only 50%
certain (so that the introduction of additional possibilities should produce
equipartition of the pie). 1! have repeatedly found, as | have written this
book, that ideas which initially struck me as absurd, pointless, &¢ have
revealed themselves to consist, upon further analysis, of unquestionable and
irreducible bases, warrants, and warrants by analogy; the future seems both
very flexible and large, and little apt to resemble the things in its past.
Distant and presumptuous critics of this book's form and content should be
prepared to listen to the author's extrabiblical defenses and to challenge him
specifically upon the points of their disagreement. The author enthusiastically
invites good criticism and a battle of wits as a healthy substitute for
critical presumptions and self-regard.

Since the manuscript is basically paginated in the order in which it has been
written, the unknowing reader is apt to find an incidence of error at the

start which is unrepresentative of the book as a whole and its final form

and conclusions. Many of these initial errors are simply monstrous since

the author began the book as an utter ignoramus on space and has proceeded to educat
himself en route. Why pay attention to ignoramuses and neophytes? One reason
is to gain an insight into one's personal ignorance. Another is that, the
author has found, the amount of thought and imagination that has so far been
given to the future of space, at least as disclosed in print, would seem,
somewhat surprisingly, minute. For this reason | have had to pioneer my

own ideas for the most part and to dismiss from annoying consideration the
extant literature, which seems so impoverished.

A particular offense is taken from the fact that the canvas of events considered |
is so lavish. It is supposed this indicates ungoverned fantasy, a defective
system for classification, rank gullibility, zero critical ability, no
scientific background, a descent into triviality, or pathological mania. |
reply that the future herself, however impossible to anticipate in specific
detail, will be fantastically lavish and complete. The complexity and variety
of the present world, which was the future of the past, is so familiar that

it goes unappreciated, even unseen. Changes in things as they are now will

be so great and fundamental that the future might seem to us improbably novel
despite a predominant resemblance to the world as it is now; these changes

need not even be basically progressive, the very kaleidoscopic flux of the
world would produce jamais vu, things incomprehensible, things spectacular,
things seemingly representative of major progress and evolution. A particular,
