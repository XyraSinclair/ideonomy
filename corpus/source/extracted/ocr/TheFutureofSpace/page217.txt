PLANETOGRAPHY. The descriptive phase of planetology; or a particular rendering
of a planet, especially its external physiography; or the methods or devices
used in planetography; or the doing of planetography itself. Either specific
to a particular planetary or that known for, or those challenges presented
by, many or all planetaries.

PLANETOLOGY. All the natural scientific aspects of one, some, or all planetaries
in the sense of what is there, what is known, &r what should be known about
their dynamics, statics, morphologies, histories, futures, etiologies, elements
chemistries, physics, biologies, meteorologies, climatologies, oceanographies,
extraplanetary influences, resources, human challenges, phenomena, similarities
and dissimilarities, classification, &rc; clearly planetology is divisible
into descriptive, theoretical, and other phases; or the equivalent ''geology'!
of such planetaries in the sense of the science of solidospheric phenomena.

~FUGAL. A suffix universally attachable to local bases (soli- for the Sun, terri- or
geo- for the Earth, luni- for the Moon, &c) to indicate flight, flow, or
motion from the locus toward extralocal Space, and therefore covering ascent
from particular bodies.

-PETAL. A suffix, converse of the preceding, denoting flight, flow, motion, or
descent toward the body indicated by the affixed base, an example being
"solipetal'' which means involving motion toward the Sun. The utility of these
2 suffixes, and the absence of specific and general synonyms, is plain.

COSMOGRAPHY. Description of the universe as a whole, any of its systems, 6&r
any of its arbitrary parts; either the process of making any or all such
description, a particular description, possible or extant description, &r
the methods and techniques of making such description. That referred to
might be the cosmos, our galaxy, our Solar System, or one particular planetary
such as Earth. Such description can embrace many things, including morphography,
rheography, historiography, chemistry, physics, plutography, biology, typology,
&r innumerable other things, however depicted. All descriptions of the
universe must ultimately wed in a consummate cosmography. The ''Myrioramic Principle"
clearly has extraordinary consequences, to the extent it is valid, for
any cosmography, either as undertaking or achievement.
