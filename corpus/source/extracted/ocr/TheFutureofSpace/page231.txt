OBJECTIONS TO SPACE

Began for passing, false, inadequate, wrong, forgotten, ambiguous, &rc reasons.

Persisted, persists, &r would persist for such reasons.

No longer popular; the vox populi calls us elsewhere.

Served its purpose.

Proved disappointing: dull, lifeless, monotonous, unvaluable, inexploitable,
difficult, unchallenging, desolate, unpromising, inferior to Earth,
too costly relative to the return, inhospitable, alien, finite, antiromantic i:
short of previous fantasies about it, programmatically impersonal,
militarily inessential, &rc.

Too expensive in absolute &r relative world, national, &r personal terms.

Interest mainly scientific, &r, yet scientific aspects and concerns sofar
or constantly neglected (the last man to leave the Moon was a scientist,
the first, and the final man to visit the Moon).

Too inflated Er self-serving.

Programs have been and promise to remain too bureaucratic, governmental,
national (rather than international or global), sectoral, &rc; operations
and progress are becoming too slow, complicated, &c.

Man cannot live well, comfortably, properly, or even subsist in Space.

Establishing man or civilization, developing resources, traveling, deriving
economic benefits, profiting scientifically, business enterprise, &rc
in terms of Space presupposes too long a beginning, too great initial
&r sustained investment, too great foresight &r planning, too great &r
maintained interest, too great a gamble, too great a profundity of
reasons, &rc to justify greater, equal, or any Spacial program.

No, inadequate, wrong, &r nonidiosyncratic spin-off on Earth.

Too much to do applicationally, conventionally, near and for Earth, more
economically, precedingly, &rc to justify other things.

The concern of posterity &r others and not ourselves.

The money, interest, science, technology, industry, resources, men, institutions,
&rc--whether governmental, corporate, or of any other nature--ought be
spent instead, if at all, on purely nonspacial things and particularly
those related to human welfare (health, recreation, and welfare; housing,
business, environment, pure and applied science and technology, deprived
and developing people, mineral and energic resources, art, religion,
education, thought, &rc); problems on Earth are too great, many, urgent, &rc.

The reason for #15 may be that these things emerged or became obvious since
the Spacial enterprise began, or perhaps because of it.

Just another problem but whose addition is unnecessary.

Few men would ever, or justifiably, want to visit &r emigrate to Space.

The Spacial program is too inhuman, arrogant, vulgar, unnatural, meaningless, &rc

Space is no solution to overpopulation and could even worsen it.

The Spacial enterprise is scientifically, technologically, economically,

&r psychically hubristic, Icarian, Faustian, Sisyphean, &r the like:
science &r technology finite or laggard, the scale of the enterprise
presupposes a far larger and more developed civilization and a much

more serious undertaking, man may be tampering with Nature, such "infinite!
scope and ambition must diminish &r corrupt us, the enterprise may be

at root fatally escapistic, man's and God's reasons alike for the Spacial
enterprise may be unknown and yet critical, all human ''progress'' should
stop, our directions and reasons for being in Space should first be
researched and decided, our programs are extravagant, other planets are
unmanageable, the Universe is malevolent, Space is a task for men centuries
removed, exponential progress is declining or will do so shortly or
inevitably (in economic, populational, &r other terms), &rc.
