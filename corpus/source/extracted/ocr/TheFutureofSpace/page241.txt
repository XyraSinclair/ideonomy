The most interesting conclusion extractable from this ramifying discussion,
itself infinite, would seem to be that the rate of mental and ontic evolution

of beings a little beyond us in past or future history might well have such
exponential characteristics and inexhaustible potentialities that time as we
know it--whether truly linear or pseudolinear (both are possible) --may
subjectively for these beings slow to limit rates of zero, become infinitesimal
in rate; so the galaxy and phanerocosm may in this way be temporally punctured
with beings who, attaining man's technical level, almost instantly fade from
our world by, paradoxically, engulfing eternity or discovering, like William
Blake, that eternity exists within any instant, a nirvanic state admittedly
quite beyond our modern comprehension. It is a little like our isochronic

world undergoes a Lorentzian time-retardation and infinite redshifting from

the transient standpoint of these asymptotic beings. Therefore it may happen
that our universe is voiceless for the simple reason that supracritical ly
advanced beings almost instantaneously pass from it mathematically, and now
populate the universe, as mute as nature, of indefinite, and perhaps infinite,
number. Who these beings are, and where they are, and what they are doing,

and why, may be questions as unanswerable or curious as whether a photon on
