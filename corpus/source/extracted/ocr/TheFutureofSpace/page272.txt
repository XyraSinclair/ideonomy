SOLIAN CIVILIZATION

Man is without question currently experiencing the birth pangs of a new and
greater civilization. “It is ironic and unfortunate that at this very moment a
large number of people around the world must be concerned that civilization is
on its deathbed. The coincidence is easy to explain. History attests that
ages of renaissance are at once ages of turmoil, despair mixed with hope, and
of the agony of an older order. Contemporaries of these transitions almost
without exception tend to lack a satisfactory appreciation of the greater
Prospects which are born in these transitions. Yet the greater prospects
represent nothing more than the Stages of growth of civilization.

Over the next 100 years the human race wil] experience an unprecedented
crisis and a metamorphosis into a new and grander being. A revolution will
occur far greater than represented by the previous 10,000 years combined.

During this period conditions in the world will in many wayS appear to grow
worse. There will be ample cause for believing that the world is going to Hell.
Terrible calamities may befall mankind. Nevertheless, as a whole conditions

of life throughout the world will be undergoing tremendous improvement. It

is highly unlikely that civilization, man, or his planet will be extinguished
or even suffer basic reversals aborting 30 years of Progress. Conditions and
attitudes in time and space will exhibit extreme mixture and alternation

of deterioristic and melioristic poles. Yet if one mood is apt to dominate

and prevail, throughout this period, it is apt to be optimism and belief in
Progress. Human nature is too Sunny to embrace a morbid outlook for more than
15 years if the case for such a negative outlook is as poor as it is momentarily.
Besides, the new order springing into existence will be far too interesting.

Innumerable trends wil] converge, culminate, and transform themselves

kind will occur as Part of the process. !t would be invaluable if mankind

unified consequences of the things to come. The great transition needs to
be justified and seen beyond.

There is a tendency to assume that the future will be so complex and surprising
that present efforts to see it as a whole, and to explain and justify it, are
