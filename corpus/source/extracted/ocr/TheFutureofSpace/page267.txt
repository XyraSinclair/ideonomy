A POSSIBLE EUGENIC EFFECT OF THE COLONIZATION OF SPACE

An observer of the contemporary world and the course of history leading up
to it is impressed with one overwhelming need so far unmet by men using the
extraordinary scientific powers now at their disposal: the need for essential
physical improvements in our human nature. Partly this is an intellectual
problem. We simply are not smart enough to solve all the problems that were
before us at the beginning of time and that have since been added. Partly it is
a problem arising from the defects in our inherited character: our natural
instincts, feelings, insensitivities, wishes, neuroses, behavior, and perhaps an
inherited view of the world. Do what we might to the externa] world, there remains
the world within ourselves as an overbearing fossil. The wonders of science
are enslaved to the dictates and whims of apes, chimerical and disharmonious
beings half angel and half beast, nature's culminating joke.

Those men who will colonize Space wil] necessarily differ from the average
composition of humanity left behind on Earth. Some of this selection will be
conscious, some inadvertent. The selectional process will] be complex and
the race resulting directly in Space and through subsequent intermarriage
will be equally complex in its physical and psychic makeup. But for any number
of reasons the Spacial population and its descendants should be superior men,
and they will certainly differ from the rest of mankind. Reinforcing these
things will be the aristocratic socioeconomic culture from which the immigrants
will likely be drawn, and also the very specific set of traits that will mark
this extract, and the influential attitudes brought along as an anticipation
of the conditions in Space and evolved on the basis of Spacial experiences as
the unique product of those extraordinary experiences. It might be argued that
in the long run migration into Space will become so easy that the former
segregative mechanisms wil] fail, but it is also possible that some considerable
selection will long continue to operate, and the prior character of the men and
civilization early arisen in Space will act as a barrier to dissimilar types
and a reconcentrator of analogous types. For example, some element of risk
or fear may long mark Space, or there may long prevail conditions unusual ly
demanding of the men moving and working there, presupposing special courage,
daring, hardihood, skill, brains, education, imagination, enterprise,
resourcefulness, ambition, curiosity, independence, adventuresomeness,
restlessness, life, &rc.

Space will be no place for those of less than average 10. Too much will
depend on individual men, on initiative, innovation, foresight, planning,
provision, &c. Broad competence will be too important. The slow and dull on
Earth simply will not volunteer. Inhabitants of slum, probably innately less
intelligent and full of character than others, will lack the enterprise or
value to enter Space, and the costs of life in Space will forbid emigration
and immigration by these people; slums themselves will have no beginning in
Space because of the quality of men there, and life on Earth will always be
a temptation to the lazy and incompetent so that a countercurrent exchange
mechanism will operate. This omission of those of subnormal intelligence,
representing 50% of mankind, will, in combination with the natural stress on
those with IQs of at least 110, produce a mean and median Spacial IQ of perhaps
120-125, apart from further intraspacial segregations such as are found, and
are so important, on Earth.

It may eventually happen that 20%-80% of Terrans wil] emigrate to Space
and thereby impoverish Terran population and render it even less attractive
to, and disdained by, higher intelligence in Space.
