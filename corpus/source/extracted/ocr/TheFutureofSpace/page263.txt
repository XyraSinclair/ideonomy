Living bacteria, worms, and finally 1-10 pound plants are discovered on Mars
operating on the basis of chemistry and structure analogous to that on Earth
but astonishingly different from a precise biological standpoint, and these
differences of the scarce but nevertheless highly differentiated and diverse
Martian microbiota, flora, and fauna quickly give rise to a lively interest
among the scientific community on Earth to investigate the biology and ecology
of Mars in great detail. The Programs to explore Mars instantly expand, and
the very early returns before 2000 are alone enormously informative. Efforts
begin to exploit the Martian Organisms as possible sources of air, food, and
even water, and to breed new varieties in superior conditions. It is concluded
almost at once that the discovery of life on bleak and seemingly lifeless

Mars has multiplied enormously the chances for other life in the galaxy

and universe. A new picture of a populous and evolutionary universe marks
mankind.

Signals are received from another Star which are without question artificial.
Literally tens of thousands of technicians in many fields concert to decode

the presumptive communications, which continue unabated. This discovery
provides clues where to look for other such signals, and what to look for,

and by 2000 no less than 4 transmissions are uncovered. Governments throughout
the world, reflecting everyone's curiosity and excitement, allocate hundreds

of millions of dollars to projects, facilities, and institutions dedicated

to discovering, mapping, puzzling out, and, if possible, using extrasolian
transmissions. As the characteristics of such transmissions are figured out

it begins to seem that a large number of the stars in the Milky Way, perhaps
millions, are sources of indefinite numbers of such communications, and there
is great interest as to what this may mean about the full pattern of civilizations
in the galaxy, and the distance of the nearest intelligent civilization to

the Sun. For some reason no such communications are found within 250 light-years.
Earth's climate is found to be changing rapidly for the worst as a result of
innumerable effects of industrial effluvia which Previously went unnoticed,

and other changes in the distribution of vegetation such as Brazilian
deforestation. Extrapolation shows that within 25-50 years human health will
be affected unless something is done. The catalysts seem to have caused

some irreversible disequilibrium, however, so that their subsequent nonproduction
is no solution to the Problem. Within 3 years it is realized that global
climate can easily be manipulated and the threat ended, partly by climatic
engineering costing $75B annually done from orbit, and it is perceived that
climate can not only be normalized and rendered immune to other foreseeable
effects of man, but that it can be made greatly superior to what it was before,
with a potential for even further enhancement and control in the decades ahead.
By 2000 the world spends $125B/y on global climatic engineering.
