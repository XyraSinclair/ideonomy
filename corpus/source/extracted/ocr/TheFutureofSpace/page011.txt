VON NEUMANN MACHINES. The journalist Adrian Berry, in his book The Next Ten
Thousand Years, A Vision of Man's Future in the Universe, discusses what
is actually the technological vision of the physicist Freeman Dyson, in
turn referring to the seminal ideas of the mathematical genius John von
Neumann. Von Neumann was concerned, before he died in 1957, with abstract
and physical entities possessing life's essential properties of
self-reproduction, multiplication, maintenance, growth, and evolution.

Such entities have come to be called ''cellular automata''. The development
and application of this field will surely be one of the most thrilling
chapters in the history of the intellect. Dyson realized that cellular
automata possess cosmoplastic (ie world changing) and cosmopoietic (ie
world creating) properties. They literally offer an infinite potential

to remake Universe. Suppose an automaton were produced that could fend

for itself anywhere in Universe, reproduce itself and do so as quickly as
possible, evolve to meet any conditions, and that somehow possessed the
ultimate ambitions of filling, conquering, and transforming Universe.
Suppose one were freed on Moon. Spreading and evolving exponentially,

it would, with remarkable speed, achieve just those incredible ambitions!
(Indeed, it is thought that what we call the natural universe may be merely
the pageantry of such automata, as has been suggested by computer scientist
Edward Fredkin.) Dyson has therefore suggested that man will one day have
created cellular automata, or artificial biota, engineered for particular,
grand purposes, such as the speedy refashioning of an entire planet, a
level of automation utterly beyond any semblance of that ours today. By
way of example, in centuries, or even mere decades, the whole atmosphere
of Venus could be terraformed.
