WHY SCIENTIFIC PROGRESS SHOULD ACCELERATE

Larger population.

Industrialization and education of the world.

Some freeing of science from narrow (industrial, governmental,
simplistic) concerns.

Intersections of fields, applications, tools, &c.

Distillation and use of cumulated knowledge.

Some awareness by supporters of the connectivity, pregnancy, and
importance of science.

Powerful and complete instrumentation, techniques, and methods.
Computer and mathematica] modeling.

Automation of research.

Increasing power of laws.

Empirical completion.

Theoretic burgeoning.

Empirical convergence.

Theoretic convergence.

Empirical and theoretic mutual convergence.

New and true motives and goals.

Work and convergence at many levels and in many systems.
Revolutionary progress in mathematics. (Eg it is conceivable that
beyond some threshold of mathematical or computer development

the mathematical power or creativity of computers will cease being
''degenerative'' and will "explode''.)

Such progress should either be greatly increasing or greatly
decreasing and the former seems the case.

Progress over the past 200 years should be at least equalled,
geometrically, by the next 200 (another rule of conservation).
Progress in fundamental science (eg discovery of many tables of
matter, union of fundamental forces, combination of constants,
neurological epistemology, artificial intelligence, topological
classification of chemicals and minerals, genetic engineering,
advances in probability and Statistics, advances in combinatorics).
Computers beginning to think, thinking, and outthinking men; removal
of the blockage to scientific Progress that covertly exists because
of the cumulating and involuting extent to which the powers of man's
mind have been exceeded.

Increasing focus of science on artificial things.

All science supplies analogies for all science.

Unification of all of science.

Proliferation of the applications of science.

Supreme status that science should and ultimately may assume in
human concerns; science as religion.

Spread of science into formerly extrascientific domains, eg psychology
parapsychology, economics, politics, law, sociology, history, fine
art, epistemology, theology, and metaphysics. Eg it may erelong
prove possible to abstractly model processes of civilization that
were formerly considered irrational or hopelessly complex, and it
may even be possible to confer some predictive power on history.

Epilogue. Of course there are sets of reasons why science should or might

decelerate that must be laid alongside these optimistic points, not the least
of which is that antiscience may check the past onrush of science and suspend
it in no-man's land, sine die. The perspective above, although overlooked,
is obvious.
