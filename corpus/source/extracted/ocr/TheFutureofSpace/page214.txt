The density and oddity of my style are the source of numerous objections

to which my only replies are that it is so that | do and would think, feel,

and write, that my nature (as my soul) are things | shall not alter, that

great density can be a lesson to the mind, that a concentration of stimuli

can be a torch to the imagination and impel one toward reality, that dense
textures can be things of great beauty and delicacy (as fine and regular

on their scale as things on our own), that the myriad associations inspired

by the texture of densities can function on a high level of contextural

analogy to adumbrate--as nothing else--features of things to come and the
aesthetic joys of such things, that something of the oddity of my style

must derive from its daedal Purpose and nature, that a too accurate and

fresh description of things can seem odd (for things are odd), that poetry

is also dense but by no means defective for it, that a kind of useful
discipline is required of the reader who would read my book, that an example

is made by the fact that law is fascinating and powerful (not intrinsically
repugnant) for its intricacy and deviation from ordinary English, that by
certain simple and instantaneous acts of generous mental resolve the surface
complexities and abnormalities of my style may be vanquished and be replaced

by cotentive enjoyment of a worthy unsuspected netherworld (my laborious fabric),
that much trouble ensues when alternative possibilities are omitted and

where the world is treated crudely, that the reader should focus on ideas

and derive from this perception and cognition the pleasures he misses or

seeks (since | have principally devoted myself in this book to the reproduction
of fact and the production of ideas, and otherwise consider myself incompetent),
that there are many things which do not yield up their treasures to a

glance but only after prolonged and essentially static labor upon their

natural facets and subtle nuances (and | consider my work an example), that

by such general, rough, various, novel, crude, indirect, connotative, and

loose treatment--by confining myself to certain things--! have achieved in

my book important suggestions of the future which could never have been
accomplished had |! limited myself to more customary and simpler means, &c.
Argumentations and ideas are presupposed by much of the text that do, or will,
appear elsewhere in the book in explicit and Pure treatments.

All strange and coined words will be defined and justified in a larger glossary
to be included in the final book but only naturally omitted during its first
composition. Certain coinages will be removed.

Slower, less ideal, adverse, alternative, and peculiar futures or scenarios
will be presented and considered in the later book.

The book will be edited and may be completely rewritten; 2 books May appear.
Many readers fault and even condemn this book because of its avoidance or
shabbiness of mathematical, physical, engineering, &r other scientific or
technical detail, substantiation, argumentation, &rc. Partly this ts due to
the limitations of current knowledge and partly it is due to my shortcomings

as an author since in innumerable cases |! do not know how to demonstrate,
develop, and criticize matters, do not know what we know, feel myself to have
walked on weak ground, and have no reportial command. I! only wish it were
otherwise! My curiosity about facts and details is immense, and who enjoys
looking a fool? My excuse for nevertheless doing what | have done is that
these things are matters of degree, someone has to be the first fool, my book
can function as a preliminary outline and a device serving further investigation,
and possibilities should be surveyed, arranged, compared, integrated, and winnowed,
