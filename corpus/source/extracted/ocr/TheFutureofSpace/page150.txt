By 2015 flywheels wil! probably be used on a large scale to store energy in
Space. The flywheels may be tremendously massive, swift, various, &r many. They
will be used in interplanetary Space, the Moon, asteroids, aboard ships, &c.

Some will be spherically compact, others discoids and toroids (the latter two
maximizing peripheral velocity or the like). They will be used in Space owing

to the low or zero gravity, extreme vacuum, storage needs, free bodies, possibly the
cryogenic temperatures, &re there. Rotors will already prevail in Space for
artificial gravity, attitudinal control, and natural reasons. Asteroids, superabundant
and of all sizes, may possess considerable natural counterrotation that might
--just imaginably--provide local energy. What is the spectrum of such spins,
momenta, collocations, &c? Asteroids, since possessed of negligible gravity, could
be brought arbitrarily close to one another. Or the counterrotation could be
artificial, a means of storage if not a source of natural energy. Fancifully,

the spin of asteroids might be used to support "vertical satellites'! (cords tied

to the surfaces of asteroids and suspended by centrifugal force, as has been
fancied for Earth), the playing out of such a cable might lift materials from
asteroids' surfaces, run generators, &c, and distant cables might even launch
projectiles at high velocities--but an arbitrarily strong cord might be essential!
The use of high-momentum flywheels on Earth has recently been reproposed for
stationary and vehicular storage of energy, yet conditions in Space would be

far more congenial. Gerard K. O'Neill has suggested that counterrotating colonial
cylinders could exchange cars in order to mimic perpetual motion in transportation.
A toroid, or just a counterweighted cable, might be gradually accelerated in
rotation in order to launch somewhat exomotive projectiles, depending upon the
strain tolerable by such a toroid, cable, or pole on the basis of familiar or
transcendental science. It has been suggested that Jupiter's daily rotation might
be accelerated in order to mine the immense planet to build a Dyson sphere. An
interesting question is what size of rotor would allow maximal installable &r
usable momentum, and whether innumerable microscopic rotors might or might not be
ideal. Two counterrotating bidimensional discs 1-100 miles in diameter, involving
very little matter (they could even amount to counterrotating I-circles made of

2 cables &c), could be placed in the sky as satellitic clocks (in fact, 48
hour-marker satellites, in 2 counterrotating cycles, could encircle the Earth

for global horology); an analogous system could provide the time to developing
nations and eliminate the millions of watches that do, and the billions that

would, exist (thereby saving billions, or an eternity, of dollars spent otherwise
on manufacture and repair, and some unknown amount by synchronizing the world's
"watchable!' peoples). (I will not, at this point, propose placing a projectable
screen in the sky to economize on movies or television; until the world is more
populous or wealthy, it would squander photons.)
