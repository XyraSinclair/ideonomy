To the extent it is ever done or found to be important, natural caves
will be discovered &r exploited on the Moon &r other planetaries by 2010.
This is a more daring prophecy than it might at first seem since the agents
and circumstances of cave formation on Earth, at least in their ordinary
senses, are considered actually or potentially inapplicable to the extraterran
planetaries, caves on which would therefore be exceptional or unpredictable.
If caves do exist elsewhere in the Solar System, questions must be asked
about their likely and possible ranges of density, size, depth, character,
interest, cause, need, use, dangers, loci, and planetaries. As they have
in Earth's past, caves might serve initial &r later inhabitation, mining
purposes, other industrial purposes (storage, processing, shelter), science, &r
simply adventurous exploration. Terran caves have biological, hydrological,
meteorological, geophysical, climatological, &r astronomical origins:
in terms of biogenic calcareous rock, water waves and currents, waves and
currents of air, vulcanism and diastrophism, precipitation, epochal glaciation
and deglaciation, stratification, seasons, days, months, groundwater, oceans
and terrestrial streams, other astronomical periods and events, &rc. Other
Terran planetaries are seen in less picturesque (in less differentiated,
active, varied, complex, powerful, and appropriately speleogenic) terms, and
jovian planetaries, being immersed in impenetrable and incalculable
atmospheres, cannot be understood or investigated speleogically (which is
not to say that any lower atmospheric fabrics, and lithospheric soils and
rock, might not generate and contain caves in these planetaries, even to
a degree unknown on Earth). Lengthy lava caves occur over the Earth that
might well be copied on other planetaries. Probably most, perhaps the
overwhelming majority of, caves on Earth are undiscovered and underexplored.
An ideal method of detection is necessary, and might alter the situation on
other planetaries. Processes destroying caves are also dominant on the Earth
whose possible absence off Earth might preserve, especially certain types of,
caves, or leave them open to the surface. What is necessary for fluctuation,
and differentiation of compositional and morphological statics and dynamics,
of planetary environments (regardless of speleogenicity)? What specific
values and correlations in terms of orbital eccentricity, diurnal period,
tilt of a planet's axis of rotation, inclination of annual orbit (if this
is important, say with respect to the extended heliosphere), various
long-term precessions and perturbations, solar irradiance or general lithospheric
and atmospheric energy and activity (including exoergic processes), atmospheric
structure (depth, stratification, composition, dynamics, &c), &c? (The
answers to such questions are essential as well to understanding the origin,
evolution, form, and varieties of life, the existence of minable deposits of
minerals, the natural interests of planetary milieux, and the scientific value
of their comprehension.) It is possible that the set of possibly influential
astronomical characteristics previously listed might identify one or another
planetary as of superior interest to the Earth in general or specific terms,
such as the terms listed. For example, the orbits of most planets (and therefore
their satellites) are far more eccentric than Earth's: Mars is 3X as eccentric,
Jupiter is 5X, Mercury is 12X, and Pluto is 15X (ecc. 0.247). The diurnal
axes of Mars, Saturn, and Neptune are slightly more inclined than the Earth's
(Uranus is incl. 98.0, making for a stabler but bizarre situation, unless its
inclination varies or varied eonically, in which case, here and elsewhere,
very interesting phenomena are imaginable; Pluto is unknown). The day of Venus,
Mercury, and Pluto is longer than ours, acquiring seasonal aspect. The day
