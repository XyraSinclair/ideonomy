All the preceding discussion runs the risk of annoying the reader since it has
nothing to say about any reasons why such an expansion of human numbers, or

such an exodus into Space, should really occur. It merely suggests, ina
phenomenological way, that certain rates and scales of transportation and resource
provision are possible and plausible on the basis of graduations that have occurred
historically. This in itself, of course, is for many people surprising. But in

the final analysis it is necessary to motivate such growth. Let me therefore

list a possible set of some alternate, additive, &r multiplicative raisons d'etre
for the various rates and scales, successive and competitive, which | have suggested.

(1) The exploration and development of frontiers has historically played a major
role in the increase of human numbers, both at the frontier and in the old
Provinces. Our subject is in some ways the greatest frontier man will have ever
faced and one might be justified in concluding that, for whatever reasons,
population will increase as the new frontier is invaded. Development of the
entire Solar System over the next }-2 centuries seems inevitable, whatever
difficulties are experiencedand however laggard the process be in the beginning;
in fact, initial hesitations may have no effect on the dates by which things are
eventually accomplished (a familiar situation in business cycles and long-term
economic growth).

(2) Once man masters his fertility, which seems imminent as a result of perfect
and universal contraception, it is likely that wholly new considerations wil]
dictate the growth of population. Current fears that the growth of population

is out of hand should largely vanish within 50-60 years, or sooner on optimistic
assumptions. [{t will then become apparent that the universe should be populated
in an ideal way. In addition to contraception, it is probable than by 2025-2050
perfect ectogenesis will replace maternal birth since the latter process wil]
then be unnecessary, inconvenient, dangerous (to mother and child), and unsuitable
for certain purposes (such as very rapid increase of population); ectogenetic
birth, and possibly nursing as well, will by then be far superior to previous
natural methods for having and raising children. Obviously the considerations
governing the stability and growth of population will be totally different than
those which dominated the world 50-75 years earlier. Any enlargement of the race

will be planned and prudent, a synthesis of larger purposes.
