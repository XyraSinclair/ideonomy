FUTURE SPACE EVENTS: EXPLANATORY NOTES

The various lists and calendars of events are presented in a preliminary
way and are meant to illustrate the nature of certain final productions. They
were, in fact, literally written in a hurry and without consultation of facts
which will later be turned to for the sake of criticism and refinement. This
is not to suggest, however, that the dates and events are not censidered or
are overly foolish. The blatant and indefensible errors are presumably rare.

The question naturally arises whether the events and dates have much
chance of being accurate, no matter how well done. Is the author gifted with
prophetic powers such that his schedule of events represents absolute
foreknowledge about the unique and unalterable course of things? The answer is
that there are an infinity of worthwhile schedules, all different, that could
be made. That is, the very attempt to confer some reasonable order and pattern
upon all possible events is beneficial and enlightening. The hypothetical
calendar created in this daring way is a temptation to all kinds of criticism,
as well as being a challenge to man's larger program. It prompts revisions
and alternatives. It feels events out in an intuitive way. It casts at least
one man's perspective in a chronological mold. Even if the exact chronology
is defective, something of the larger order and pattern may not be. Events
in the future of space are clearly extraordinarily contingent upon political,
military, social, technical, and purely spacial developments. Yet there is an
opportunity for devising a sequence fairly independent of such contingencies
whose pace and sequencing is educational and persuasive, and can to some extent
be relied on in the face of such (frustrative or acceleratory) contingencies.

The events and dates are useful in another important respect. They
supply a story to, and dramatize, what may in fact or in effect occur. On
a scale of possibilities within 200 years an evolutionary imagery has the effect
of justifying nearer events by relating them to a larger, more impressive, and
more meaningful framework. Space exploration and development is shown to have
a start and a proper continuation. Maintenance and growth of man's spacial
enterprise is rendered sensible. Initial exploration and development are
identified with important consequences. Sadly, this has never been done. It
has been made to seem that things done in space and yet to be done are smal}
and arbitrary. The result has been minor interest in space programs and
unreliable support. The space program in general has yet to fulfill itself
and the nature of fulfillment has never been depicted.

"A General Calendar for the Future of Space'': comments on. Each "event"!
designated in this calendar might as well be prefaced by the words: ''If the
event is going to occur, it might reasonably occur by...'' Such a thought
doubtless preoccupied me as | dated and arranged the events. There was
obviously, also, a competition between date-of-feasibility and date-of-actual-
occurrence, a conflict which mars the overall chronology. As long as the
user remains sensible of such factors, however, their effect is not apt to
be catastrophic, and the calendar will serve its imperfect role.

It is noteworthy that on the longer scale of time the calendar may be
more realistic since the extrinsic and unpredictable factors influencing
spacial events in the nearer term, say within 50 years or by 2025, may lose
something of their turbulence and subside after 2025 and/or smoothed over bigger
units of time.
