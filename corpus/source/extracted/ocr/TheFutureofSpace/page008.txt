Is the pace of events set forth in my calendars too slow or too hasty? |
concede at once that it may happen that mankind, over the next few decades, will
basically turn its back on outer space and concern itself with more down-to-Earth
problems and opportunities. Whereas | do not think this will happen, | do think
there will be 1-2 or 1-3 decades of restraint over the world, and some ignoration
of larger possibilities. Also, there are a set of likely or unlikely things which
might restore or enlarge an interest in space, say over the next 3 decades, and
impose themselves on the natural background of temporary neglect.

The pace of events | have assumed presupposes exponential progress in human
affairs, generally, and to some extent also in space. As such it is merely
consistent with continued exponential growth or a reasonable logistic curve
moderating future growth. Even if world demographic and economic growth begins
to turn over and obey a logistic curve in the future, there is good reason to
think that scientific progress will lag or disobey such an inflection and will
continue to accelerate or maintain approximately its current rate, and certainly
there are some affairs (of which the space program may prove an example) which
should behave in this optimistic way.

If the exploration and development of outer space continues in a serious and
vigorous way, the rate of change and evolution | have given to events is perfectly
reasonable. Modern progress in science, technology, engineering, and other human
accomplishment is really extraordinarily fast. Both prospectively and retrospectively
it is almost incomprehensible. Were space pursued with enthusiasm or abandon
my calendar would probably prove to have been very conservative. The timing of
the entire calendar, therefore, is critically dependent upon a good estimate of
future human motivation and whatever things this, in turn, is dependent on.
