The new colonies established throughout the Solar System during the 2]st century
and thereafter will to some extent be what | have metaphorically referred to as
"edens"'. That is, they will represent the founding of various independent
civilizations, histories, populations, biospheres, microcosms, races, &c and
will potentially or actually be self-supporting and not zero-sum entities, or
"redundant earths''. Thelr basic materials may be the common soil and other
materials that exist in such incredible plenty as the bodies of Solian planetaries,
and their basic energies may be the overabundant light of the Sun which is
currently wasted, or those in the future taken from the nuclei of virtually all
elements, however common. Beyond this they will amount to plutographic, industrial,
sociological, and biological wholly or virtually wholly closed ecosystems or
vivaria, and not be such as to for Practical purposes deplete finite supplies
of Terran or Solian resources, pollute these realms, or threaten the destiny and
greater purpose of our star. They will thus constitute demonstrational models
of man's ability and likelihood of surmounting supposed evils of continued
exponential growth and crises foreseen for the future of the world by current
advocates of the limits of growth. In these ways, and by sparing the state
of things on Earth, such colonies in Space as anticipated, or when built, will
appeal to environmentalistic sentiments since Earth wil] be isolated from Space
and might even be deindustrialized by finished imports from the colonies in
Space. In away it is hard to imagine any finer or more idealistic deed than
sprinkling new civilizations in Space. Such civilizations themselves being
self-reproducible, and a fantastic or perhaps infinite universe remaining beyond,
the initial deed by Terrans, whatever combination of semiconscious motives behind
it, might be an act of infinite worth, the greatest thing the human race, or
ourselves as the initiators of the process, could and will ever do. This
extraordinary fact deserves close study since it is truly extraordinary and perhaps
amounts to the miraculous.

It has been remarked that there are no Martians because we human beings have
yet to launch the Martian civilization that wil] give rise to the enchanting
inhabitants of that planet long foreseen by romantic fiction. Likewise the rest
of the universe appears uninhabited by superior beings because our destiny of
filling the universe with superior beings evolved from ourselves has yet to
fulfill itself, and perhaps because this saturation will be a very fast and
unidirectional process amounting to a cosmic phase transition and preempting
other and independent life. Thus it can be seen that someone must take the
initiative in realizing the potentialities of the universe and hesitations about
the colonization of Space are naive in disregarding infinite consequences of actions
and the apparent relative importance of these consequences for everything we
might otherwise do. Effects of our present actions on the future lives of our
children already exert a potent influence as governors of our actions, a situation
expressing in miniature a possible greater concern with posterity such as proposed
here as a new obligation of mankind.
