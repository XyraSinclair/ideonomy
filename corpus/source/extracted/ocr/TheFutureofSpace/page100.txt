Being visualized is a vehicle that would leave and pursue the magnetic poles

--or pilot magnetospheric flux lines, isomagnetics, and radii--of the Earth,
Jupiter, Sun, Milky Way, &rc by means of Atlantean on-board permanent magnets

or electromagnets. For example, a set of such magnets could be arranged in
reversible radial symmetry, &r in connexion with redistributable insulation,

SO as to create in effect an artificial magnetic monopole of macroscopic
dimension whose reversible polarity would propel the craft as already described,
possibly almost isentropically, The critical acceleration possible in this

way would have to be calculated. The notion of a spaceship operating by

magnetic levitation intraspacially &r subspacially is common in science fiction
but otherwise seems hard to realize. Levitation over the surface of a planet,

by magnetic or other analogous means, is actually plausible if fantastic

kinetic &r potential energy could be realized, contained, and controlled aboard
some superscientific craft at some future date, and the idea that beings visiting
Earth in flying saucers from another star would possess means seeming to defy
gravitation and momentum (by exhibiting seemingly infinite acceleration, orthogonal
turns, isentropic levitation, and infinite power) is probably correct, and will
probably hold when our civilization lands intelligent life on another star

in the 22nd century. Magnetic fields and currents may have an important role

in the structure, dynamics, and energic densities of the Universe, in which

case they may have a similar importance in the future navigation of the Universe;
but all this is rank surmise. Geomagnetism has been used for attitudinal

control in satellites. Much greater magnetism surrounds Jupiter and certain
Stars. Some planetaries possess much less magnetism than Earth. Certain

radical scientific and technologic developments in magnetism may well eventuate
in 25-100 years and transvalue it.

By 2005 there will be used as sources of energy such things as volcanoes,
geothermal gradients, petrochemicals (presumably inorganically produced), other
combustibles, &r other endergonic reactants on &r in the Moon &r other planetary
bodies. Flashes have occasionally been seen on the Moon that have been interpreted
as vulcanic eruptions. Active volcanoes usable for the generation of much power
may well obtain throughout the Solar System, and even on many larger satellites.
These may play an important or critical role in the initial ér later exploration
&r development of Solar planetaries. On at least one of them vulcanism may even
be more active than on Earth, for example it is conceivable that Jupiter's Great
Red Spot overlies and discloses some extraordinary, however unorthodox, volcano.
Terran lithospheric ''plumes'', such as are conjectured to exist, may amount to
immense volcanoes, as may as well Earth's orogenic and midoceanic ridges, in
which case analogous thermal sources may exist and be exploitable on other
planetaries as technology advances and the need arises. The steepness of crustal
thermal gradients on other planetaries is unknown, but it is noteworthy that the
integrated heat flux of the Earth's surface equals all the energy from fuels man
uses today, and that the flux is so heterogeneously distributed over the surface.
It has long been surmised that Venus may have a "hydrology''--an ocean or lakes--
based on a chemical other than water; there is a chance this could have some
energic significance on or off Venus. Liquid fuel chemicals might obtain as
ultradeep oceans on the Jovian planets. The importance of living organisms,
geophysics, and conditions analogous to those on Earth, in terms of the past or present
establishment of sources of energy usable by man, are all largely unknown. The
point is that we really don't know what energies exist elsewhere in the Solar System
as resources exploitable by man sooner or later in the development of our system.

In China, on the Amur River below Yermakavo, lie the Burning Mountains, which

are rocky precipices made up of complex layers of spontaneously igniting carbonaceous,
clayey shales that are continually steaming and sometimes give off flames! Who

knows how complex and varied the Solar System may be.
