One foresees fissionables being mined on the Moon by 2015, as already discussed.
Presumably these would be the ones already mined on Earth and presumably these
exist on the Moon in equal abundance. Whether they compare with Earth as to the
surface or depth of these bodies--and compare in amount, concentration, and access~~
is another matter; the Moon might be superior or inferior in these respects. What
margin of superiority or accessibility would be necessary to justify the economics
of their mining, in competition with alternate sources of energy, is unknown; the
question must be asked both about direct use on the Moon and distant importation
by the Earth or extralunar Space. Prior epilunar industrial concentration might
be critical or important to the economics of extralunar use owing to pound costs
of available transportation systems. Further, even more abundant, concentrated,
&r convenient fission &r fusion fuels may, sooner or later, be found elsewhere
in the Solar System and even on Earth. Remarkable technology may come about for
planetographic detection, survey, and quantitation of natural fissionables, 6&r
for extraction &r refinement of ores--and these developments may be absolutely
decisive for the patterns of resource use. Such technology will almost certainly
eventuate to some extent. The discovery and large-scale extraction, processing,
and use of these Lunar fissionables may play a key role in the emergence of
Lunar civilization--industry, population, and culture--between 2015-2050. What
should be done is calculations made for the cost of that industry essential to
