One of the greatest reasons for the inevitability of the development of Space,
especially its colonization, is the susceptibility of men in general to the
fanatical beliefs of certain minds always appearing in society, perhaps especially
in the future, eager to make their case and easy at persuading others, first to
listen, then to wonder, then to commit themselves mind, body, and soul to the
new gospel. It is obvious that the wild, romantic, and challenging claims of
these sometimes questionable and sometimes unquestionable fanatics wil] galvanize
large parts of mankind and lead many sheep into the sky. As important as anything
in the makeup of human history has been the influence of symbolism and the power
of ideas. Long journeys and arduous undertakings have continually been begun
on foolish cause which was later unimportant before the accidental benefits of
the enterprise and the creativity ignited in those who set themselves free. In
an age of leisure spared the necessity of work the motility of humanity may be simply
enormous and the colonization of Space may prove an irresistible recreation,
creed, or trial.
