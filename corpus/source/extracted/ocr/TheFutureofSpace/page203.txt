BLEAK SCENARIO. In this scenario the current falling support of NASA and the
conservatism of the Soviets' programs continues. Congress views Space as a
dead issue and is merely concerned with its careful dismantlement. NASA
remains, after 1983, as an impotent fossil, most of its chores having been
turned over to the military. The Space Age proves to have been truly meteoric:
as soon born as dead. Nevertheless, fossi1 NASA, absorptive military, and
foreign programs (themselves drastically restrained where not abol ished)
continue to support spacial activities on a level commensurate with inflation,
and thus $10B are spent on Space in 2000. However, Quarter | turns out to
have been a morbid, feeble, dull, and slow time indeed. This bleak Quarter
could be described as being followed by a revival, along various lines, or

the 21st century might be painted in similarly somber terms. The wrongness
of this bleak treatment of Space might be illustrated by comparison with
nonbleak scenarios or by mentioning directly in bleak scenarios what is lost
in terms of the spirit of man, a world that might have been, science and
technology ignored, and trends on Earth encouraged and isolated.
